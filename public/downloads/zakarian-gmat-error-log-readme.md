# Zakarian GMAT — Error Log Template

This is the spreadsheet I used to climb from 565 to 735. Six columns, one row per missed question, two months of honest logging. The patterns surface on their own once you have 60–80 entries.

## How to use it

1. **Open the CSV** in Google Sheets, Excel, or Numbers. Three example rows are pre-filled — delete them or use them as a reference.
2. **Log every miss for at least 14 days before drawing conclusions.** Single rows tell you nothing. Aggregates tell you everything.
3. **Use only one tag per row.** If a miss feels like two tags, pick the *upstream* one — the cause that, if fixed, would have prevented the rest.
4. **At week 3, sort by tag.** Pivot table or filter, doesn't matter. You will find that 70–80% of your misses come from two tags. Those two are your study plan for the next month.
5. **At week 6, re-test.** Pick five flagged questions per dominant tag, attempt them cold, mark Y/N in the re-test column. The ones you still miss tell you the fix didn't take.

## The six tags

- **Conceptual** — you didn't know the rule. Fix: focused reading + 5 questions on the rule within 48 hours.
- **Careless** — you knew the right answer; you computed it wrong. Fix: slow down on the *last* step of every problem.
- **Time pressure** — you knew the right answer; you ran out of time. Fix: per-question time limits in practice. Train the cutoff.
- **Misread** — you answered a different question than the one being asked. Fix: underline the question word every time.
- **Strategy** — you used the wrong approach; the right one would have been faster. Fix: in review, identify the *fastest* approach, not just the right one.
- **Other** — anything that doesn't fit. Use sparingly. If "Other" is more than 10% of your log, your taxonomy is too narrow.

## What this template doesn't do

It doesn't surface patterns automatically. It doesn't space the re-tests for you. It doesn't tell you which chapter to open next. The platform — zakariangmat.com — does all of that.

If you want to run the loop in a spreadsheet, this is the loop. If you'd rather have it run for you, the platform is free while it's in private beta: enter an official-practice-exam baseline and it builds your weak-area plan and spaced review from there.
