# Zakarian GMAT — Outreach Playbook

Manual, personalized, one-to-one outreach. Every message below is a starting
point, not a script to paste. Edit each one so it sounds like you talking to that
specific person. If you would not send it to a friend, do not send it.

Companion files: `GROWTH_PLAN.md` (strategy + metrics), `LAUNCH_ASSETS.md`
(public posts), `REFERRAL_AND_DISCOUNT_PLAN.md` (the offer mechanics).

---

## 1. Principles

- **Help first, sell second.** Open by being useful (a study tip, an offer to look at their plan), not by pitching.
- **Personalize the first line.** Reference something real about them — their post, their target school, the last time you spoke. Generic openers read as spam.
- **One person at a time.** No automation, no scraping, no copy-paste blasts, no LinkedIn bots. This protects your reputation and complies with platform rules.
- **Short.** A DM should be 3-6 sentences. Long pitches get ignored.
- **One ask, soft.** End with a low-friction question ("want me to send it?"), not a hard close.
- **No fake claims, ever.** No guaranteed scores, no invented results, no implied GMAT/GMAC affiliation. Cite only your verified 565 to 735.
- **Take no for an answer.** One polite follow-up max if there is no reply. Never a third.

---

## 2. Who to message, and how to find them (ethically)

Pull only from people you actually have a relationship or a legitimate connection with:

- **Segment A — Prior advice-askers:** search your own DMs/WhatsApp/email for "GMAT". These are warmest.
- **Segment B — Warm friends / BI + b-school peers:** your real classmates and study contacts.
- **Segment C — 1st-degree LinkedIn connections** who are MBA/MiM applicants or in admissions/consulting/finance.
- **Segment D — Club / community admins** you can reach respectfully.
- **Segment E — Early users** (for referrals and feedback), once they exist.

Daily volume: **4-6 personalized messages**, not more. Quality over volume. Space
them out; do not blast 30 at once.

---

## 3. Personalization checklist (before you hit send)

- [ ] First line references something specific and true about them.
- [ ] The message would make sense even if they had never heard of the product.
- [ ] There is a clear, small, optional next step.
- [ ] No guarantee language, no fake urgency, no copy-paste tell.
- [ ] You actually want to help this person (if not, skip them).

## 4. The templates

### Template 1 — Warm friend / client

*(email subject): Built a GMAT thing — want a look?*

> Hey [name] — random one. I finally finished building the GMAT prep platform I kept talking about. It's the exact system I used to go from 565 to 735, turned into something structured.

*Note:* acknowledge you haven't spoken — don't fake familiarity. If you have nothing true to reference, don't send it.

### Template 8 — Follow-up after they started the beta but went quiet

> Hey [name] — saw you got into the platform, nice. Did you manage to get a baseline in and see your study plan? That's the part that makes the rest click.
>
> If you want, I'll take 10 minutes and walk you through setting it up and reading your weak areas — just say the word.

*Note:* the activation moment is entering an official-exam baseline. A personal offer to help here is the single highest-leverage message in the funnel.

### Template 9 — Conversion message after positive feedback

> That's great to hear, [name] — exactly what I built it for. Since you're getting real use out of it: while we're in beta you can lock in a founding rate — a permanent 30-40% discount for whenever paid plans turn on. No charge now, you're just reserving the early price.

> Want me to add you to the founding list? (For you specifically I can do the founding rate at $300 if that's easier.)

*Note:* only after genuine positive feedback. The $300 special is your discretion — use it for hand-picked, high-value people, not everyone.

---

### Template 10 — Referral message after a purchase / founding sign-up

> Thank you for backing this early, [name] — it genuinely means a lot. One thing: if you know anyone else prepping, your founding access comes with a referral. They get the founding discount and you get $50 when they join.

> Copy-and-send message is here: [/refer]. Zero pressure — only people you'd actually recommend it to.

*Note:* the moment right after someone commits is the best time to ask for a referral. Make it about helping their friends, not extracting from them.

## 5. Cadence and timing rules

- **4-6 personalized messages/day**, spaced out.
- **One follow-up max** per non-reply, ~4-6 days later.
- Reply to every response **within a couple of hours** during launch.
- Work the warmest segments first (A, then B), then C-D as warm lists run low.
- If a segment's reply rate is under ~10%, stop and rethink the message before sending more.

## 7. Do / Don't

**Do**
- Personalize every first line.
- Offer to set up their study plan or walk them through their baseline.
- Use the $300 special warm-user price sparingly, for the right people.
- Send referrals the `/refer` link so it's effortless.

**Don't**
- Paste the same message twice without editing.
- Use any automation/scraping/bulk-DM tool.
- Promise scores or imply GMAT/GMAC affiliation.
- Drop links into groups without the admin's blessing.
- Send a second follow-up. One is enough.
