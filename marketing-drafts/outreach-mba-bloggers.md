# Outreach — MBA admissions bloggers and consultants

**Status:** draft, awaiting Adam's review.

**Goal:** Get the platform mentioned (or in the consultant's "tools I recommend" section) by 3-5 reputable MBA admissions consultants in the next 90 days. Each meaningful mention can drive sustained, qualified traffic — admissions consultants' audiences are exactly the platform's ICP.

**Why this works:** Admissions consultants advise their clients on GMAT score targets every single week. They are constantly asked "which prep platform should I use?" and most of them either default to whatever has affiliate revenue (TTP, Manhattan) or punt the question. A founder-built, narrative-driven platform with a real story is differentiated enough to be recommendable.

---

## Where to find them

- **Poets & Quants** — comment authors and contributors. Most have their own consulting practice linked.
- **Stacy Blackman, Menlo Coaching, mbaMission, Personal MBA Coach, Fortuna Admissions** — large players. Tier 1.
- **Independent consultants on LinkedIn** — search "MBA admissions consultant" with 1k+ followers. Tier 2.
- **YouTube channels** — Touch MBA, MBA Voices, MBA & Beyond. Tier 3 (lower priority because video is harder to embed a platform mention into.)

## Filtering criteria

Target consultants who:
- Have written publicly about GMAT score thresholds, score targets, or test prep choices in the last 18 months.
- Specialize in M7/T15 admissions (audience matches the platform's "ambitious 700+" positioning).
- Have a non-trivial audience (1k+ LinkedIn followers, 2k+ newsletter subscribers, etc.).
- Are not already employed by a competing prep company.

Skip:
- Generic "all-purpose" consultants with no GMAT angle.
- Anyone running a prep company themselves (conflict of interest).
- Anyone whose primary marketing channel is paid ads (they don't recommend things; they sell their own service).

## Cadence

- 2-3 outreach emails per week. Lower volume than tutor outreach because consultants are senior and less responsive to cold contact.
- Personalize the FIRST AND SECOND paragraphs of every email. Generic outreach to consultants gets ignored or reported.
- Follow up exactly once, 10-14 days after first contact.

## Email — first contact

Over the last year I built a GMAT prep platform around the system I used to do it. It's deliberately the opposite of the content-heavy platforms — error-log-driven, with an adaptive study plan that reorganizes itself around your weakest patterns and a spaced review queue that resurfaces past misses. No video lectures. No generic curriculum. The chapter readings exist; the work happens in the review layer.

I'd like to give you full platform access, no strings attached, so you can evaluate whether it's something you'd want to mention to your audience. If it's a fit, I can build out an affiliate or referral structure that works for you. If it's not, I'd genuinely welcome your honest feedback on what's missing for the audience you serve — that feedback alone is worth the cold email.

The platform is at zakariangmat.com — happy to send a complimentary access link if you're open to a look.

Adam

## What to send AFTER they say yes

If they reply asking for access:

1. Email them within 4 hours with a complimentary access code or pre-provisioned account.
2. Include a one-page "what to look at first" guide:
   - Take the diagnostic (5 min) — see the per-topic report.
   - Open one chapter (e.g. Critical Reasoning) — see how it differs from a video course.
   - Look at the error log + study plan — these are the two most differentiated surfaces.
3. Do NOT chase them for a write-up. Let them make their own decision in their own time. Following up to ask "did you write about it yet?" is the fastest way to get unsubscribed from.

If they ghost after access:
- One short email at the 30-day mark: "Hi — just checking if you had any thoughts after looking at the platform. Genuinely not chasing for a recommendation; only after honest feedback. Either way, hope your year is going well."
- Then drop it.
