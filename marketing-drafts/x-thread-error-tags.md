## Tweet 1 (hook)

Most GMAT students study wrong.

They do 50 practice questions and feel productive.
They get 20 wrong, check the answers, and move on.
Three weeks later: same mistakes, same score.

The fix isn't more practice.
It's building a real error log.

Here's the 6-tag system I used to go from 565 to 735 🧵

## Tweet 2

Tag 1 — CONCEPTUAL.

You don't know the underlying rule.

Example: missed a DS question because you forgot that you can't divide both sides of an inequality by a variable without knowing its sign.

Fix: 15 min of focused reading on the rule. Then 5 questions on it within 48 hours.

## Tweet 3

Tag 2 — CARELESS.

You knew the right answer. You computed it wrong.

Example: missed an Algebra question because you wrote -3 instead of -2 in your scratch work.

Fix: not "be more careful." Slow down on the LAST step of every problem. Most carelessness lives there.

## Tweet 4

Tag 3 — TIME PRESSURE.

You knew the right answer. You ran out of time before you got there.

Example: skipped the last Verbal question because the previous CR ate 4 minutes.

Fix: per-question time limits in practice. Train the cutoff, not the speed.

## Tweet 5

Tag 4 — MISREAD.

You answered a different question than the one being asked.

Example: a CR question asks what would WEAKEN the argument. You answered with what would strengthen it.

Fix: underline the question word every single time. Yes, every single time.

## Tweet 6

Tag 5 — STRATEGY.

You used the wrong approach. The right one would have been faster.

Example: tried to solve a Two-Part Analysis algebraically when plugging values from the answer list would have taken 30 seconds.

Fix: review what the FASTEST approach would have been, not just the right one.

## Tweet 7

Tag 6 — OTHER.

Anything that doesn't fit the five above.

Use sparingly. If "Other" is more than 10% of your log, your taxonomy is too narrow. Add a sixth meaningful tag instead of letting "Other" do the work.

## Tweet 8

After two months of logging, sort the log by tag.

You will find that 70-80% of your misses come from two tags. Almost no one's distribution is even.

Once you know which two, you stop drilling content. You drill the BEHAVIOR that produces those two tags.

## Tweet 9

That's the whole thing.

Six tags. One row per missed question. Two months of honest logging. Then study the tag distribution, not the topics.

This is the system that took me from 565 to 735 — non-native English, no engineering background, working full-time.
