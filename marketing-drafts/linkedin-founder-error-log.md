## Body

I scored 565 on my first GMAT.

Not a typo. 565.

I wasn't bad at math. I wasn't bad at English. I was bad at reviewing.

Every wrong answer, I'd check the solution and move on. Three weeks later, I'd get the exact same question type wrong.

What changed: I started keeping an error log. Not a vague "I need to work on CR" note — a structured log. Was it conceptual? Careless? Time pressure? Misread the prompt? Strategy? Something else?

After 60 days, I could see my actual patterns. Five error types were responsible for 80% of my wrong answers. Two of them I'd never even noticed because they were spread thinly across topics.

Once I started fixing those five things instead of "doing more practice," my mock scores moved fast.

Final score: 735. 100th percentile.

Eight months from 565 to 735, as a non-native English speaker, with no engineering background, while working full-time.

The error log was the single highest-leverage habit. Every other study tactic stacked on top of it.

If you're stuck somewhere in the 600s, it is almost never a content problem. It is almost always a review problem. Happy to share the six-tag taxonomy I used — just ask in the comments.
