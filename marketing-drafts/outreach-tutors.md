# Outreach — Independent GMAT tutors

**Status:** draft, awaiting Adam's review.

**Goal:** Build a small (5-10) referral network with independent GMAT tutors. They refer students to the platform; the platform pays a referral fee or gives the tutor a discounted bulk seat to resell.

**Why this works:** Most independent GMAT tutors are 1-person businesses with no analytics infrastructure, no question bank, and no error-log tooling. They teach methodology in 60-min sessions and tell students to "drill more" between sessions. A platform that pairs with their teaching is a value-add for them and a meaningful revenue line for Adam.

---

## Where to find them

- **GMAT Club tutor directory** — every tutor with a verified profile. Filter to ones with 50+ posts (means they're active, not abandoned).
- **r/GMAT** top contributors who explicitly mention they tutor.
- **YouTube** — search "GMAT tutor [topic]" and find creators with 5k-50k subs. Smaller channels respond more.
- **LinkedIn** — search "GMAT tutor" or "GMAT coach" with the location filter set to your target market.

**Avoid:** the big prep companies (Manhattan, Magoosh, TTP). They have their own platforms and won't refer out.

## Filtering criteria

Send only to tutors who:
- Have a public scoring story OR a verified GMAT score listed publicly.
- Tutor independently (not employed by a big-prep company).
- Show recent activity (posted in the last 60 days).
- Are based in a market where Adam wants to grow (English-speaking primary; non-native-friendly secondary).

Skip:
- Anyone whose marketing leans hard on "guaranteed 700+ in 4 weeks" — different segment, doesn't fit the platform's positioning.
- Anyone who white-labels another platform already.

## Cadence

- Send 3-5 outreach emails per week. More than that is a job.
- Personalize the first paragraph for every email. The second through fifth paragraphs can be templated.
- Follow up exactly once, 8-10 days after the first email. After that, drop it.

## Email — first contact

**Subject:** Quick question about the way you teach DI

Hi [Name],

I came across your [specific piece — name it] on [specific platform — name it] and the way you broke down [specific point — name it] genuinely changed how I'd think about teaching that topic.

I'm Adam Zakarian. I went from 565 to 735 over eight months as a non-native English speaker with no engineering background, and I've spent the last year building a GMAT prep platform around the system I used to do it. The platform pairs structured chapters with an error-log analytics layer, an adaptive study plan, and a question bank — basically the systematic infrastructure that lets a tutor focus on coaching instead of content delivery.

I'm building a small network of independent tutors I can refer students to and partner with — referral fee or discounted bulk seats, depending on what works for you. The pitch from your side: your students get a platform that reinforces what you teach in session, and you get a recurring revenue line on top of your hourly rate.

Would you be open to a 20-minute call sometime in the next two weeks? No deck, no pitch. Just a look at the platform and an honest conversation about whether there's a fit.

The platform is at zakariangmat.com if you want to poke at it first.

Adam

## Tracking

Maintain a simple spreadsheet for outreach. Columns:
- Name / handle / platform
- First contact date
- Follow-up date
- Reply (yes/no/maybe)
- Outcome (call booked / declined / no response / partnership signed)
- Notes

Review weekly. Reply rate of 15-25% is normal; book rate of 30-40% on replies is normal. If reply rate is below 10% after 20 emails, the personalization isn't deep enough.
