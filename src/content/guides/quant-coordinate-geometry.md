# Quant Coordinate Geometry Micro-Guide

## Introduction

Coordinate geometry is the GMAT's pure-geometry surrogate after the Focus Edition removed traditional plane geometry from Quant. Lines, slopes, distances, and the occasional circle make up the content. The skill is fluency with the formulas and recognizing which apply.

I score 735s on this test. Coordinate geometry is the subskill where formula recall and formula application are everything — there's not much room for clever insight beyond knowing the right tool.

*All worked examples are my own writing*, original to this guide.

## The Coordinate Plane

The xy-plane has horizontal x-axis and vertical y-axis intersecting at the origin (0, 0). Points are denoted (x, y).

### Quadrants

I (upper right): x > 0, y > 0.
II (upper left): x < 0, y > 0.
III (lower left): x < 0, y < 0.
IV (lower right): x > 0, y < 0.

## Lines

### Slope-intercept form

y = mx + b, where m is slope and b is y-intercept.

### Slope between two points

m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁).

### Point-slope form

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).

### Standard form

Ax + By = C, where A, B, C are constants.

### Special slopes

Horizontal line: m = 0. Equation: y = constant.
Vertical line: m undefined. Equation: x = constant.

## Parallel and Perpendicular

### Parallel

Same slope. Different intercepts (or coincident if same).

### Perpendicular

Slopes multiply to −1 (negative reciprocals).

If a line has slope 2, the perpendicular slope is −1/2.
If a line has slope 0 (horizontal), the perpendicular has undefined slope (vertical).

## Distance and Midpoint

### Distance formula

Between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂):

d = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²].

This is the Pythagorean theorem applied to the horizontal and vertical separations.

### Midpoint formula

Midpoint of (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂):

M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).

Component-wise average.

## Circles

### Circle equation

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r², where (h, k) is the center and r is the radius.

### Circle from definition

A circle is the set of points equidistant from the center.

## Intersection of Lines

Two non-parallel lines intersect at exactly one point. Solve the two equations as a system.

## Ten Worked Examples

### Worked example 1 — slope from two points

*Problem.* What is the slope of the line passing through (3, 1) and (7, 9)?

*Solve.* m = (9 − 1) / (7 − 3) = 8/4 = 2.

*Answer: 2.*

### Worked example 2 — equation of line

*Problem.* Find the equation of the line with slope 3 passing through (2, 5).

*Solve.* y − 5 = 3(x − 2). y = 3x − 6 + 5 = 3x − 1.

*Answer: y = 3x − 1.*

### Worked example 3 — perpendicular slope

*Problem.* What is the slope of a line perpendicular to y = 4x + 7?

*Solve.* Slope of given is 4. Perpendicular slope: −1/4.

*Answer: −1/4.*

### Worked example 4 — distance

*Problem.* What is the distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6)?

*Solve.* d = √[(4−1)² + (6−2)²] = √[9 + 16] = √25 = 5.

*Answer: 5.*

### Worked example 5 — midpoint

*Problem.* What is the midpoint of (−2, 5) and (8, 1)?

*Solve.* ((−2 + 8)/2, (5 + 1)/2) = (3, 3).

*Answer: (3, 3).*

### Worked example 6 — circle equation

*Problem.* What is the equation of the circle with center (3, −2) and radius 5?

*Solve.* (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25.

*Answer: (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25.*

### Worked example 7 — intersection of lines

*Problem.* Find the intersection of y = 2x + 1 and y = −x + 7.

*Solve.* 2x + 1 = −x + 7. 3x = 6. x = 2. y = 5.

*Answer: (2, 5).*

### Worked example 8 — line through origin

*Problem.* Find the equation of the line through (0, 0) and (4, 8).

*Solve.* Slope: 8/4 = 2. Through origin: y = 2x.

*Answer: y = 2x.*

### Worked example 9 — does point lie on line

*Problem.* Does the point (3, 7) lie on the line y = 2x + 1?

*Solve.* y = 2(3) + 1 = 7. Yes.

*Answer: Yes.*

### Worked example 10 — perpendicular bisector

*Problem.* Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of the segment from (1, 2) to (5, 8).

*Solve.* Midpoint: (3, 5). Slope of segment: (8−2)/(5−1) = 6/4 = 3/2. Perpendicular slope: −2/3. Equation: y − 5 = −2/3 (x − 3). y = −2x/3 + 2 + 5 = −2x/3 + 7.

*Answer: y = −2x/3 + 7.*

## Coordinate Geometry Trap Compendium

### Trap 1: Slope sign error

A line from lower-left to upper-right has positive slope; lower-right to upper-left has negative slope.

### Trap 2: Vertical line slope

Vertical line has undefined slope (not zero). Horizontal has zero (not undefined).

### Trap 3: Distance formula sign

Squaring eliminates sign concerns, but write out (x₂ − x₁)² carefully.

### Trap 4: Midpoint as average

Component-wise average. Don't mix x and y.

### Trap 5: Perpendicular slope formula

Negative *reciprocal*. m₁ × m₂ = −1.

### Trap 6: Circle center sign

(x − h)² where center is at h, so if center is (3, −2), it's (x − 3)² + (y + 2)².

### Trap 7: Distance squared vs distance

If problem says "distance squared," don't take a square root.

### Trap 8: Intersection mistake

Set the two y-expressions equal, solve for x; then plug back to find y.

### Trap 9: Line equation form confusion

y = mx + b vs Ax + By = C. Convert as needed.

### Trap 10: Reading the figure

GMAT figures often aren't to scale. Use given values, not visual estimates.

### They use slope formula reflexively.

(y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁).

### They know perpendicular = negative reciprocal.

Reflex.

### They use distance formula as Pythagorean.

√[(Δx)² + (Δy)²].

### They use midpoint as component-wise average.

((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).

### They write circle equation correctly.

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r².

### They label points and compute carefully.

Avoid sign errors.

### They convert line forms freely.

Slope-intercept ↔ point-slope ↔ standard.

Verify.

Use given values.
