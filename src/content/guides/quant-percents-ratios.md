# Quant Percents and Ratios Micro-Guide

## Introduction

Percents and ratios appear in roughly 30% of Quant problems and dominate certain DI formats. The content is elementary; the trap density is among the highest of any subskill. A student fluent in percent operations and ratio-scale thinking saves dozens of seconds per problem and avoids classic distractor traps.

## Percent Fundamentals

### The three notations

Percent, decimal, and fraction are three notations for the same quantity. Fluency in switching is the core skill.

20% = 0.20 = 1/5.
75% = 0.75 = 3/4.
12.5% = 0.125 = 1/8.

### Memorized conversions

Cold-memorized:

1/2 = 50%, 1/3 ≈ 33.3%, 2/3 ≈ 66.7%, 1/4 = 25%, 3/4 = 75%, 1/5 = 20%, 2/5 = 40%, 3/5 = 60%, 4/5 = 80%, 1/6 ≈ 16.7%, 5/6 ≈ 83.3%, 1/7 ≈ 14.3%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 3/8 = 37.5%, 5/8 = 62.5%, 7/8 = 87.5%, 1/9 ≈ 11.1%, 1/10 = 10%, 1/12 ≈ 8.3%.

### Computing percent quickly

10% of N: move decimal one place left. 10% of 347 = 34.7.
1% of N: move decimal two places left. 1% of 347 = 3.47.
5%: half of 10%. 5% of 347 = 17.35.
20%: double 10%.
25%: divide by 4.
50%: divide by 2.

### The percent flip

x% of y = y% of x. 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 4. Useful when one side is cleaner.

## The Critical Distinction: Percent Greater vs Percent Of

### "Percent greater than" (or "more than")

If B is 25% greater than A, then B = A + 25% of A = 1.25A.

### "Percent of"

If B is 25% of A, then B = 0.25A. Here B is smaller — it's a fraction of A.

These are different — confusing them is a top-five Quant error pattern.

### Examples

"X is 30% more than Y" → X = 1.30Y.
"X is 30% of Y" → X = 0.30Y.
"X is 30% less than Y" → X = 0.70Y.
"X has decreased by 30%" → new = 0.70 × old.
"X has increased by 30%" → new = 1.30 × old.

## Percent Change

(new − old) / old × 100%.

Percent change is always relative to the original (base) value.

### Successive percent changes

Multiplicative, not additive. A 30% gain followed by a 30% loss:

Final = 1.30 × 0.70 = 0.91, a 9% net decrease.

Not zero. The classic trap.

### General formula

For successive multipliers (1 + p₁), (1 + p₂), ..., final = original × Π(1 + pᵢ).

### Percent change in reverse

If a quantity grew by 25% to reach 250, what was the original?

Original × 1.25 = 250. Original = 250 / 1.25 = 200.

Going back: divide by 1 + percent. Going forward: multiply by 1 + percent.

## Ratios

### What a ratio is

A ratio compares two or more quantities. 3:5 means for every 3 units of the first, there are 5 units of the second. The ratio carries only relative information.

### Part to whole

If the ratio of A to B is 3:5, then A is 3/(3+5) = 3/8 of the total, and B is 5/8.

### Scaling

Express ratios as ak, bk, ck. The k is a scaling constant determined by an absolute anchor in the problem.

### Compound ratios

If A:B = 3:2 and B:C = 4:5, find A:B:C. Scale so the B in both ratios matches.

A:B = 3:2 = 6:4 (multiplied by 2). B:C = 4:5.

A:B:C = 6:4:5.

### Continuing a ratio

If A:B:C = 2:3:5 and the smallest of A, B, C is 4, find the total.

The smallest is A = 2k = 4, so k = 2. Total = (2 + 3 + 5)k = 10 × 2 = 20.

## Mixture / Alligation

When mixing two solutions to get a target concentration, the ratio of the amounts is given by alligation:

Mix solution 1 (concentration c₁) with solution 2 (concentration c₂) to get concentration c. Ratio of amounts = (c₂ − c) : (c − c₁), with each amount paired with the *opposite* far distance.

Example: mix 30% acid with 60% acid to get 50% acid. Ratio of (30%) to (60%) = (60 − 50) : (50 − 30) = 10 : 20 = 1 : 2. So 1 part of 30% to every 2 parts of 60%.

## Ten Worked Examples

### Worked example 1 — basic percent

*Problem.* What is 35% of 280?

*Solve.* 10% = 28. 30% = 84. 5% = 14. 35% = 84 + 14 = 98.

*Answer: 98.*

### Worked example 2 — percent change

*Problem.* A price increased from \$80 to \$92. What is the percent increase?

*Solve.* (92 − 80) / 80 × 100 = 12 / 80 × 100 = 15%.

*Answer: 15%.*

### Worked example 3 — successive percent change

*Problem.* A company's revenue grew 40% in year 1 and declined 25% in year 2. What is the net percent change?

*Solve.* Final / original = 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05. Net: +5%.

*Answer: 5% increase.*

### Worked example 4 — percent reverse

*Problem.* A book is on sale for \$45, which is 25% off the original price. What was the original price?

*Solve.* Sale price = 75% of original. So original = 45 / 0.75 = 60.

*Answer: \$60.*

### Worked example 5 — percent greater than

*Problem.* X is 60% greater than 25. What is X?

*Solve.* X = 1.60 × 25 = 40.

*Answer: 40.*

### Worked example 6 — percent of with reverse

*Problem.* 20 is 25% of what number?

*Solve.* 20 = 0.25 × N. N = 20 / 0.25 = 80.

*Answer: 80.*

### Worked example 7 — basic ratio

*Problem.* The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 2:3. If there are 12 boys, how many girls are there?

*Solve.* boys = 2k = 12, so k = 6. girls = 3k = 18.

*Answer: 18.*

### Worked example 8 — ratio with anchor

*Problem.* The ratio of red to blue marbles is 4:7. If there are 15 more blue than red, how many marbles are there in total?

*Solve.* red = 4k, blue = 7k. Difference: 7k − 4k = 3k = 15, so k = 5. Red: 20, blue: 35. Total: 55.

*Answer: 55.*

### Worked example 9 — compound ratio

*Problem.* A:B = 3:4 and B:C = 5:6. Find A:B:C.

*Solve.* Scale so B is consistent. A:B = 3:4 = 15:20. B:C = 5:6 = 20:24. A:B:C = 15:20:24.

*Answer: 15:20:24.*

### Worked example 10 — alligation

*Problem.* How many liters of a 20% acid solution must be added to 30 liters of a 50% acid solution to produce a 35% acid solution?

*Solve.* Ratio of (20%) to (50%) = (50 − 35) : (35 − 20) = 15 : 15 = 1 : 1. So equal amounts. Add 30 liters of 20%.

*Verify.* 30 × 0.20 + 30 × 0.50 = 6 + 15 = 21. Total volume 60. Concentration: 21/60 = 35%. ✓

*Answer: 30 liters.*

## Percent and Ratio Trap Compendium

### Trap 1: Percent greater vs percent of confusion

"30% more than X" = 1.30X. "30% of X" = 0.30X.

### Trap 2: Successive percent additive

30% + 20% ≠ 50%. Multiplicative: 1.30 × 1.20 = 1.56, a 56% increase.

### Trap 3: Percent base wrong

When a price increases by 20% then decreases by 20%, the second percent applies to the new price, not the original.

### Trap 4: Reverse percent

If a price after a 25% increase is 100, the original is 80, not 75 (don't subtract 25%).

### Trap 5: Ratio without anchor

Treating 3:5 as actual quantities (3 and 5) instead of as a scale.

### Trap 6: Compound ratio without aligning B

Need to make the bridging variable equal in both ratios before combining.

### Trap 7: Part-to-part vs part-to-whole

3:5 part-to-part. The fraction of total that's the first part is 3/8, not 3/5.

### Trap 8: Alligation reversed

Pair each amount with the *opposite* distance, not the same.

### Trap 9: Conversion-decimal slip

50% as 0.5, not 5.0 or 0.05.

### Trap 10: Discount applied wrong

20% off \$100 = \$80, not \$20.
