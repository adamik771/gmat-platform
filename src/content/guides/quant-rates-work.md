# Quant Rates and Work Micro-Guide

## Introduction

Rates and work problems share the same underlying structure: *amount = rate × time*. Whether the problem involves distance traveled, work completed, water flowing into a tank, or words typed, the math is the same. Yet students often treat these as different problem types and get tangled when the wording shifts. The skill is recognizing the underlying structure regardless of context.

I score 735s on this test. Rates and work problems are where I rarely lose points because I've internalized two principles: *rates add when objects act simultaneously* and *time is what you usually have to solve for*. This guide teaches both.

*All worked examples are my own writing*, original to this guide.

## The Core Identity

amount = rate × time.

In every rates problem, you have or want to find some combination of these three quantities.

Distance = speed × time.
Work = work-rate × time.
Volume = flow-rate × time.

Pick the variable you're solving for, fill in the other two from the problem, compute.

## Units Discipline

Every rate has units: per unit of time. Speed is miles per hour or kilometers per second. Work-rate is jobs per hour or words per minute. Volume rate is liters per minute or gallons per second.

If the problem mixes time units (hours and minutes, seconds and minutes), convert to a single unit before computing. Mixing units is the most common rate error.

## Combined Rates

When multiple objects act simultaneously, their rates add.

If Anne paints at 1/6 house per hour and Bob paints at 1/9 house per hour, together they paint at 1/6 + 1/9 = 5/18 house per hour. Time to finish 1 house: 18/5 = 3.6 hours.

### Combined-rate worked example

*Problem.* Pipe A fills a pool in 4 hours; Pipe B fills it in 6 hours. How long if both work together?

*Solve.* Combined rate: 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 pools per hour. Time: 12/5 = 2.4 hours.

*Answer: 2.4 hours.*

### Pipe-fill-and-drain

When one object adds and another subtracts (filling pipe + draining pipe), one rate is negative.

If A fills at 1/6 and B drains at 1/9, net rate: 1/6 − 1/9 = 3/18 − 2/18 = 1/18 per hour. Time to fill: 18 hours.

If B drains faster than A fills, net rate is negative, and the tank never fills.

## Relative Motion

Two objects moving in the same direction or opposite directions.

### Opposite directions

If two objects move toward each other, closing speed = sum of speeds. Time to meet = (initial distance) / (closing speed).

### Same direction (chasing)

If A is ahead of B, both moving in the same direction, B catches up at speed = (B's speed − A's speed). Time to catch = (gap) / (closing speed).

If B is slower, B never catches A.

### Same direction (escaping)

If A is ahead and moves faster than B, gap grows. The gap-growing speed is (A's speed − B's speed).

## Average Speed

The average speed for a journey with multiple legs is *total distance / total time*. Not the average of the speeds.

If you drive 60 mph for the first half of a journey and 40 mph for the second half:

Let half-distance be d. Time for first half: d/60. Time for second half: d/40. Total time: d/60 + d/40 = 2d/120 + 3d/120 = 5d/120 = d/24. Total distance: 2d. Average speed: 2d / (d/24) = 48 mph.

NOT 50 mph (the simple average).

This is the most common rate-problem trap. Memorize: average speed for two equal-distance legs is the harmonic mean: 2v₁v₂/(v₁ + v₂).

## Ten Worked Examples

### Worked example 1 — basic distance-rate-time

*Problem.* A car travels at 50 mph for 3 hours. How far does it travel?

*Solve.* Distance = 50 × 3 = 150 miles.

*Answer: 150 miles.*

### Worked example 2 — solving for time

*Problem.* A train covers 240 miles at 80 mph. How long does the trip take?

*Solve.* Time = distance / rate = 240 / 80 = 3 hours.

*Answer: 3 hours.*

### Worked example 3 — combined work

*Problem.* Worker A completes a task in 5 hours; Worker B in 10 hours. How long together?

*Solve.* Combined rate: 1/5 + 1/10 = 2/10 + 1/10 = 3/10 task per hour. Time: 10/3 ≈ 3.33 hours.

*Answer: 10/3 hours.*

### Worked example 4 — opposing motion

*Problem.* Two trains start 360 miles apart and travel toward each other at 50 mph and 70 mph. After how many hours do they meet?

*Solve.* Closing speed: 50 + 70 = 120 mph. Time: 360 / 120 = 3 hours.

*Answer: 3 hours.*

### Worked example 5 — chasing motion

*Problem.* A train traveling at 40 mph leaves at noon. A second train, traveling at 60 mph in the same direction, leaves the same station at 1 pm. At what time does the second train catch the first?

*Solve.* By time the second leaves, first has 40-mile lead. Closing speed: 60 − 40 = 20 mph. Time to close: 40/20 = 2 hours. So second catches first at 1 pm + 2 hours = 3 pm.

*Answer: 3 pm.*

### Worked example 6 — average speed

*Problem.* A driver covers a distance in two parts: half at 60 mph and half at 90 mph. What is the average speed for the trip?

*Solve.* Let half-distance = d. Time first half: d/60. Time second half: d/90. Total time: d/60 + d/90 = 3d/180 + 2d/180 = 5d/180 = d/36. Total distance: 2d. Average speed: 2d / (d/36) = 72 mph.

*Answer: 72 mph.*

### Worked example 7 — fill and drain

*Problem.* Pipe A fills a tank in 5 hours; Pipe B drains the tank in 8 hours. If both are open and the tank starts empty, how long to fill the tank?

*Solve.* Net rate: 1/5 − 1/8 = 8/40 − 5/40 = 3/40 tanks per hour. Time: 40/3 ≈ 13.33 hours.

*Answer: 40/3 hours.*

### Worked example 8 — three workers

*Problem.* If A can complete a task in 6 hours, B in 8 hours, and C in 12 hours, how long if all three work together?

*Solve.* Combined rate: 1/6 + 1/8 + 1/12 = 4/24 + 3/24 + 2/24 = 9/24 = 3/8 task per hour. Time: 8/3 ≈ 2.67 hours.

*Answer: 8/3 hours.*

### Worked example 9 — varying rate

*Problem.* A typist types 60 words per minute for the first 30 minutes, then her pace slows to 40 words per minute for the next 30 minutes. What is her average rate over the full hour?

*Solve.* Total words: 60 × 30 + 40 × 30 = 1800 + 1200 = 3000 words. Total time: 60 minutes. Average: 50 words per minute.

*Answer: 50 wpm.*

### Worked example 10 — joint effort with one starting late

*Problem.* Anne can complete a job in 4 hours; Bob can complete the same job in 6 hours. Anne works alone for 1 hour, then Bob joins. How long does the entire job take?

*Solve.* Anne's rate: 1/4. Anne's first hour: 1/4 of the job done. Remaining: 3/4. Combined rate: 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12. Time for remaining: (3/4) / (5/12) = (3/4) × (12/5) = 36/20 = 9/5 hours = 1.8 hours. Total time: 1 + 1.8 = 2.8 hours.

*Answer: 2.8 hours.*

## Rates and Work Trap Compendium

### Trap 1: Average of speeds for varied-leg trips

Average speed is total distance / total time, not arithmetic mean of speeds.

### Trap 2: Adding times instead of rates

If A takes 4 hours and B takes 6 hours, together they don't take 10 hours. Rates add, then convert.

### Trap 3: Unit mismatch

mph and minutes mixed without conversion.

### Trap 4: Missing the late start

When one worker starts before another, account for the head start.

### Trap 5: Inverted rate

If "Anne paints 1 house in 4 hours," Anne's rate is 1/4 house per hour, not 4.

### Trap 6: Fill-and-drain sign

When one pipe drains, its rate is subtracted, not added.

### Trap 7: Negative net rate

If drain exceeds fill, the tank never fills. Watch for this.

### Trap 8: Distance vs displacement

In a round trip, total distance is twice the one-way distance, but displacement is zero. Average speed uses total distance.

### Trap 9: Different units across legs

If first leg is in miles and second in kilometers, convert.

### Trap 10: Round-trip with different speeds

Use harmonic mean: 2v₁v₂ / (v₁ + v₂) for equal-distance round trips.

## Pacing for Rates and Work

Standard target: 1:30 to 2:00 per problem.

For combined-rate problems, the structure is mechanical once recognized. Most should take well under 2 minutes.

For multi-leg or relative-motion problems, sketch the situation if it helps. The 30 seconds invested in a clear setup beats 2 minutes of confusion.

## Study Protocol

### Week 1: Basic rate computation

Drill 10 problems per day on basic distance-rate-time and work-rate.

### Week 2: Combined rates

### Week 3: Relative motion

### Week 4: Average speed

For combined work.

### They use closing speed for relative motion.
