## @detail-workflow

Specific detail questions ask about explicitly stated facts: "According to the passage, which of the following is true of Y?" or "The passage states that Z is responsible for which of the following?"

**Mental model.** This is an open-book lookup, not a memory test. The passage is right there on the screen. Your job is to navigate to the right lines fast — your structural map tells you which paragraph — then verify a light paraphrase. Answering from memory is the single biggest avoidable error on this type.

**The template.** The answer is literally in the passage — not paraphrased into a new claim, but directly supported by a specific line.

**The workflow.**

1. Read the question. Identify the specific topic (a noun phrase: "customer acquisition costs," "silk road traders," "mycorrhizal networks").
2. Scan the passage for that topic. Find the paragraph(s) that address it.
3. Re-read that paragraph carefully.
4. Match to the answer that is directly supported by those lines.

**The re-read discipline.** You SHOULD re-read for specific-detail questions. That's the whole point — these questions test careful reading of specific parts, and the passage stays on screen exactly so you can re-read. Don't try to answer from memory; scan, find, verify.

**The five-second scan.** Your eye can often find a noun phrase in the passage in 5 seconds, especially since RC passages aren't long. Scan, don't re-read everything.

**The "close paraphrase" trick for the right answer.** The correct answer usually doesn't use the exact words from the passage — it paraphrases lightly. This is deliberate. The GMAT wants to test whether you understood the content, not whether you can match keywords. So the right answer often looks *less* like the passage than a trap that parrots its vocabulary.

> **Recall check.** State the four-step detail workflow from memory. Now: on a detail question, is the answer that reuses the passage's exact wording more or less likely to be correct than one that paraphrases it? (Answer: less likely — exact-word answers are often the word-match trap, while the correct answer usually paraphrases. Verify by meaning, not by vocabulary.)

## @detail-traps

Wrong detail answers cluster into three shapes. All three are defeated by the same move: point to the exact line and check that it means the same thing.

**The word-match trap.** Wrong answers often repeat *specific words* from the passage but rearrange them into a claim the passage doesn't make. Example: passage says "costs rose 60% over five years." Trap answer: "costs have risen by about 60% over the past decade." Same words, wrong time frame.

**The "consistent with" trap.** Wrong answers are sometimes consistent with the passage but not *stated* by it. Specific Detail questions require stated, not merely compatible, information. If a claim "seems true" given the passage but isn't supported by specific lines, it's wrong here — though it might be right on an inference question.

**The "partial match" trap.** An answer that captures half of what the passage says is still wrong. "The passage states X and Y." If an answer says only X, it's incomplete. Look for the answer that fully matches.

**Micro-drill.** The passage states: "Solar installations in the region rose 40% between 2018 and 2021, driven mainly by falling panel prices." For each answer, mark supported, or name the trap. 60 seconds:

1. "Solar installations in the region rose about 40% over a three-year span." → ___
2. "Solar installations in the region rose 40% per year." → ___
3. "Falling panel prices are the most important driver of solar adoption worldwide." → ___

Answers: (1) **Supported** — 2018 to 2021 is three years; light paraphrase of the stated fact. (2) **Word-match / distortion** — recasts a *total* rise as an *annual* rate. (3) **Consistent-with / out-of-scope** — the passage said "in the region" and "mainly," not "worldwide" and "most important"; it's a generalization the text doesn't support.

**Trap to watch.** Don't bring in outside knowledge. You might *know* that subscription businesses have a 75% retention rate — but if the passage says 70%, the answer matching the passage is right, regardless of reality. RC tests what the passage says, not what's true.

## @summary

Specific detail is an open-book lookup. Navigate with your map, re-read the lines, and verify a light paraphrase against the text.

**The process.**

1. Identify the topic noun phrase in the question.
2. Scan to the paragraph; re-read the lines.
3. Pick the answer whose *meaning* matches — usually a light paraphrase, not a word-for-word echo.
4. Reject word-match, merely-consistent, and partial-match answers.

**What to do next.** Drill the problem sets below; treat every miss as a failure to go back to the text, and re-do it by citing the exact supporting line. Next chapter: **Inference** — where the answer is no longer stated in the passage but must follow from it.
