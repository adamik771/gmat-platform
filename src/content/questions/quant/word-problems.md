## Q1
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Rate/Time/Distance

Two friends start at opposite ends of an 18-mile trail and walk toward each other simultaneously. Maria walks at 4 mph and James walks at 2 mph. How many hours after they begin will they meet?

- A) 2
- B) 3
- C) 4.5
- D) 6
- E) 9

**answer:** B
**explanation:** This is a rate-time-distance problem involving two objects moving toward each other. The governing principle is that when two travelers move toward each other along the same path, the distance separating them decreases at a rate equal to the sum of their individual speeds.

Let t represent the number of hours after the two friends begin walking until they meet. Maria walks at 4 miles per hour, so in t hours she covers 4t miles. James walks at 2 miles per hour, so in t hours he covers 2t miles. Because they start at opposite ends of the 18-mile trail and walk toward each other, the sum of the distances they cover must equal the full length of the trail when they meet. Therefore 4t + 2t = 18.

Combining like terms gives 6t = 18, and dividing both sides by 6 yields t = 18/6 = 3. Equivalently, the two friends close the gap at a combined rate of 4 + 2 = 6 miles per hour, so the time to close the 18-mile gap is 18/6 = 3 hours.

The correct answer is B.

## Q2
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Profit/Loss

A merchant buys a product for $40 and sells it for $52. What is the merchant's profit as a percentage of the cost?

- A) 12%
- B) 20%
- C) 23%
- D) 25%
- E) 30%

**answer:** E
**explanation:** Profit expressed as a percentage of cost is the profit divided by the cost, then multiplied by 100. The base of this percentage is the cost, not the selling price.

The cost is $40 and the selling price is $52, so the profit is the selling price minus the cost: 52 - 40 = 12 dollars.

Dividing the profit by the cost and converting to a percentage gives (12 / 40) x 100 = 0.30 x 100 = 30 percent. Therefore the merchant's profit, measured as a percentage of the cost, is 30 percent.

The correct answer is E.

## Q3
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Age Problems

A father's age is currently three times his daughter's age. The sum of their current ages is 52 years. How old will the father be in 5 years?

- A) 39
- B) 41
- C) 44
- D) 47
- E) 52

**answer:** C
**explanation:** This problem is solved by translating the two stated relationships into a system of linear equations, solving for the present ages, and then applying the required time shift.

Let d represent the daughter's current age. Because the father's age is currently three times the daughter's age, the father's current age is 3d. The sum of their current ages is 52, which gives 3d + d = 52, that is, 4d = 52. Dividing both sides by 4 yields d = 13. So the daughter is 13 and the father is 3d = 3(13) = 39.

The question asks for the father's age in 5 years, not his current age, so we add the 5-year offset: 39 + 5 = 44.

The correct answer is C.

## Q4
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Work Rate

A printer can print 240 pages in 8 minutes. At this rate, how many pages can it print in 15 minutes?

- A) 360
- B) 400
- C) 420
- D) 450
- E) 480

**answer:** D
**explanation:** Because the printer works at a constant rate, this is a uniform-rate problem in which the number of pages produced equals the rate of printing multiplied by the time spent printing. The governing relation is pages = rate x time, so we first determine the printing rate and then apply it to the new time.

Let r represent the printing rate in pages per minute. We are told that the printer produces 240 pages in 8 minutes, so

r = 240 / 8 = 30 pages per minute.

We now apply this constant rate to a time of 15 minutes. Let p represent the number of pages printed in 15 minutes. Then

p = r x time = 30 x 15 = 450 pages.

Thus the printer can print 450 pages in 15 minutes.

The correct answer is D.

## Q5
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Rate/Time/Distance

Train A leaves Station X heading east at 70 mph. Two hours later, Train B leaves Station X heading east at 90 mph. How many hours after Train B departs will it overtake Train A?

- A) 5
- B) 6
- C) 7
- D) 8
- E) 9

**answer:** C
**explanation:** This is a pursuit problem in which one object overtakes another moving in the same direction. The governing principle is that the faster object closes the distance between them at a rate equal to the difference of their speeds, and the time required to overtake equals the initial separation divided by that closing rate.

Let t represent the number of hours after Train B departs at which it overtakes Train A. First we determine how far apart the trains are at the moment Train B leaves. Train A travels east at 70 miles per hour and departs two hours before Train B, so during those two hours Train A covers 70 multiplied by 2, which equals 140 miles. Thus, when Train B departs, Train A is 140 miles ahead.

Because both trains travel east, Train B gains on Train A at the difference of their speeds. The closing rate is 90 minus 70, which equals 20 miles per hour. Train B overtakes Train A when it has erased the 140-mile head start, so we require that 20 multiplied by t equals 140. Solving for t gives t equals 140 divided by 20, which equals 7.

The correct answer is C.

## Q6
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Work Rate

Machine A can complete a job in 6 hours. Machine B can complete the same job in 12 hours. If both machines work together, how many hours will it take to complete the job?

- A) 3
- B) 4
- C) 5
- D) 6
- E) 9

**answer:** B
**explanation:** When two machines work together on the same job, their individual rates of work add. A rate of work is the fraction of the job completed in one unit of time, and it is the reciprocal of the time required to complete the whole job alone.

Let the entire job equal 1. Machine A completes the job in 6 hours, so its rate is 1/6 of the job per hour. Machine B completes the same job in 12 hours, so its rate is 1/12 of the job per hour.

Working together, the combined rate is the sum of the two rates:

1/6 + 1/12 = 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4 of the job per hour.

Let T be the number of hours the two machines need to complete the job together. Since rate multiplied by time equals the work done, and the work done is 1 whole job, we have

(1/4)(T) = 1.

Solving for T gives T = 4.

The correct answer is B.

## Q7
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixture

A chemist has 10 liters of a 30% acid solution. How many liters of pure acid must be added to create a 50% acid solution?

- A) 2
- B) 3
- C) 4
- D) 5
- E) 6

**answer:** C
**explanation:** This is a mixture problem in which a pure substance is added to a solution. The governing principle is that the amount of acid present, divided by the total volume of the mixture, equals the concentration of the mixture. When pure acid is added, that quantity increases both the amount of acid and the total volume of the mixture.

Let x represent the number of liters of pure acid that must be added. The original solution contains 10 liters at a 30% concentration, so the amount of acid initially present is 0.30 multiplied by 10, which equals 3 liters. After x liters of pure acid are added, the amount of acid becomes 3 + x liters, and the total volume of the mixture becomes 10 + x liters. We require the resulting concentration to equal 50%, so we set the new fraction equal to 0.5:

(3 + x) / (10 + x) = 0.5

Multiplying both sides by (10 + x) gives:

3 + x = 0.5(10 + x)
3 + x = 5 + 0.5x

Subtracting 0.5x and 3 from both sides yields:

0.5x = 2

Dividing both sides by 0.5 gives x = 4. Therefore 4 liters of pure acid must be added.

The correct answer is C.

## Q8
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Profit/Loss

A retailer marks up the cost of goods by 40% and then offers a seasonal discount of 10% on the marked price. What is the retailer's profit as a percentage of cost?

- A) 20%
- B) 24%
- C) 26%
- D) 30%
- E) 36%

**answer:** C
**explanation:** Successive percentage changes combine multiplicatively rather than additively, because each change applies to the result of the previous one. The selling price is therefore obtained by multiplying the cost by the markup factor and then by the discount factor.

Because the question asks only for a ratio of profit to cost, we may assign a convenient value to the cost. Let the cost equal $100.

A markup of 40% means the marked price is 100% + 40% = 140% of the cost. Thus the marked price is 100 × 1.40 = $140.

A seasonal discount of 10% is taken off the marked price, so the selling price is 100% − 10% = 90% of the marked price. Thus the selling price is 140 × 0.90 = $126.

The profit is the selling price minus the cost: 126 − 100 = $26.

Expressed as a percentage of the cost, the profit is 26 / 100 = 26%.

The correct answer is C.

## Q9
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Rate/Time/Distance

A cyclist travels from Town A to Town B at 15 mph and returns by the same route at 10 mph. What is the cyclist's average speed, in miles per hour, for the entire round trip?

- A) 11
- B) 12
- C) 12.5
- D) 13
- E) 13.5

**answer:** B
**explanation:** Average speed is defined as total distance divided by total time; it is not, in general, the arithmetic average of the individual speeds. Because the cyclist covers the same route in each direction, the two legs are equal in distance but unequal in time, and the total time must be computed directly.

Let d represent the one-way distance, in miles, from Town A to Town B. The round trip therefore covers a total distance of 2d miles.

For the outbound leg at 15 miles per hour, the time required is d/15 hours. For the return leg at 10 miles per hour, the time required is d/10 hours. The total time for the round trip is

d/15 + d/10.

Expressing both terms over the common denominator 30 gives

2d/30 + 3d/30 = 5d/30 = d/6 hours.

The average speed for the entire round trip is the total distance divided by the total time:

(2d) / (d/6) = 2d × (6/d) = 12.

The variable d cancels, so the result does not depend on the actual length of the route. The average speed is 12 miles per hour.

The correct answer is B.

## Q10
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Work Rate

Pipe A can fill a tank in 4 hours, and Pipe B can fill the same tank in 6 hours. Pipe C can drain the full tank in 12 hours. If all three pipes are open simultaneously, how many hours will it take to fill the empty tank?

- A) 2
- B) 3
- C) 4
- D) 5
- E) 6

**answer:** B
**explanation:** This is a combined work-rate problem. The governing principle is that rates of work add together when agents operate simultaneously, with filling rates counted as positive and draining rates counted as negative. Each rate is expressed as the fraction of the tank completed in one hour, namely the reciprocal of the time the agent needs to complete the job alone.

Let the capacity of the tank be one full tank. Pipe A fills the tank in 4 hours, so its rate is 1/4 of a tank per hour. Pipe B fills the tank in 6 hours, so its rate is 1/6 of a tank per hour. Pipe C drains the full tank in 12 hours, so it removes 1/12 of a tank per hour; because draining opposes filling, this rate is subtracted.

We compute the net rate when all three pipes operate at once:

Net rate = 1/4 + 1/6 - 1/12.

Expressing each fraction with the common denominator 12 gives 3/12 + 2/12 - 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3 of a tank per hour.

Let T be the number of hours required to fill the empty tank. Since rate multiplied by time equals the work completed, and the work to be completed is one full tank, we have (1/3)(T) = 1, so T = 3.

The correct answer is B.

## Q11
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixture

A grocer mixes Brand A coffee at $12 per pound with Brand B coffee at $8 per pound to create a blend that sells at $9.50 per pound. If the grocer makes 40 pounds of the blend, how many pounds of Brand A coffee are used?

- A) 10
- B) 12
- C) 15
- D) 18
- E) 20

**answer:** C
**explanation:** This is a mixture problem, in which the total cost of the blend equals the sum of the costs of its components. The governing relationship is that price per pound multiplied by the number of pounds gives the total cost, and the total cost of the two brands must equal the total cost of the blend.

Let a represent the number of pounds of Brand A coffee used, and let b represent the number of pounds of Brand B coffee used. Because the grocer makes 40 pounds of the blend, the quantities satisfy

a + b = 40.

Brand A costs $12 per pound and Brand B costs $8 per pound, so their combined cost is 12a + 8b dollars. The 40 pounds of blend sells at $9.50 per pound, for a total value of 9.50 multiplied by 40, which equals 380 dollars. Setting the combined cost equal to the value of the blend gives

12a + 8b = 380.

From the first equation, b = 40 - a. Substituting this expression into the second equation yields

12a + 8(40 - a) = 380.

Expanding the left side gives

12a + 320 - 8a = 380.

Combining like terms gives

4a + 320 = 380.

Subtracting 320 from both sides gives

4a = 60,

and dividing both sides by 4 gives

a = 15.

Thus the grocer uses 15 pounds of Brand A coffee.

The correct answer is C.

## Q12
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Age Problems

Five years ago, James was 4 times as old as his nephew. In 3 years, James will be twice as old as his nephew. How old is James now?

- A) 21
- B) 25
- C) 29
- D) 33
- E) 37

**answer:** A
**explanation:** Age problems are translated into algebra by assigning a variable to each person's present age and then expressing every past or future age as that present age adjusted by the stated number of years. The same time shift must be applied to all individuals named in a given condition.

Let J = James's present age and let N = the nephew's present age.

The first condition concerns the situation five years ago, when each age was 5 less than the present age. Since James was then 4 times as old as his nephew, we have:

J - 5 = 4(N - 5)
J - 5 = 4N - 20
J = 4N - 15

The second condition concerns the situation in three years, when each age will be 3 more than the present age. Since James will then be twice as old as his nephew, we have:

J + 3 = 2(N + 3)
J + 3 = 2N + 6
J = 2N + 3

Both expressions equal J, so we set them equal to each other and solve for N:

4N - 15 = 2N + 3
2N = 18
N = 9

Substituting N = 9 into J = 2N + 3 gives:

J = 2(9) + 3 = 21

Thus James is now 21 years old. As a check, five years ago James was 16 and his nephew was 4, and 16 = 4 times 4; in three years James will be 24 and his nephew will be 12, and 24 = 2 times 12. Both conditions are satisfied.

The correct answer is A.

The sum of these three integers is 72. We translate this condition into an equation:

(x minus 2) plus (x minus 1) plus x equals 72.

Since x represents the largest of the three integers, the largest integer is 25. As a check, the three consecutive integers are 23, 24, and 25, and their sum is 23 plus 24 plus 25, which equals 72, as required.

## Q14

**topic:** Averages

The average (arithmetic mean) of 5 numbers is 18. If a sixth number is added and the new average is 20, what is the sixth number?

- A) 22
- B) 24
- C) 28
- D) 30
- E) 32

**answer:** D
**explanation:** The average (arithmetic mean) of a collection of numbers equals their sum divided by the count of numbers, so the sum of the numbers equals the average multiplied by the count. We apply this relationship to both the original collection and the expanded collection.

Let S be the sum of the original 5 numbers. Since their average is 18, we have S = 5 × 18 = 90.

Let x be the sixth number that is added. After it is included, there are 6 numbers whose sum is S + x, and the new average is 20. Therefore the sum of the 6 numbers is 6 × 20 = 120.

We now translate this into an equation and solve for x:

S + x = 120
90 + x = 120
x = 120 − 90
x = 30

The correct answer is D.

Ten years ago, Sara was twice as old as her brother Tom. Today, the sum of their ages is 62. How old is Sara now?

- A) 32
- B) 36
- C) 38
- D) 40

**explanation:** Age problems of this kind are solved by translating each verbal statement about a point in time into an algebraic equation, taking care to adjust every person's age by the same time shift.

Let S represent Sara's age today and let T represent Tom's age today. Ten years ago each of them was 10 years younger, so Sara's age was S - 10 and Tom's age was T - 10. The statement that ten years ago Sara was twice as old as Tom translates into the equation S - 10 = 2(T - 10).

Expanding the right side gives S - 10 = 2T - 20, and adding 10 to both sides gives S = 2T - 10.

The statement that the sum of their present ages is 62 translates into S + T = 62.

Substituting S = 2T - 10 into this equation gives (2T - 10) + T = 62, which simplifies to 3T - 10 = 62. Adding 10 to both sides gives 3T = 72, and dividing by 3 gives T = 24.

Since S = 2T - 10, we have S = 2(24) - 10 = 48 - 10 = 38.

The correct answer is C.

## Q16
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Coin Problems

A piggy bank contains only nickels and dimes. If the bank contains 48 coins worth $3.60 in total, how many dimes are in the bank?

- A) 18
- B) 20
- C) 22
- D) 24
- E) 28

**answer:** D
**explanation:** This is a mixture problem involving two unknown quantities, which we solve by setting up a system of two linear equations: one expressing the total count of items and one expressing the total value. To avoid decimal errors, we measure all values in cents rather than in dollars.

Let n represent the number of nickels and let d represent the number of dimes in the bank.

Because the bank contains 48 coins in all, the count equation is

n + d = 48.

A nickel is worth 5 cents and a dime is worth 10 cents, and the total value of $3.60 equals 360 cents. The value equation is therefore

5n + 10d = 360.

We solve the count equation for n, obtaining n = 48 − d, and substitute this expression into the value equation:

5(48 − d) + 10d = 360.

Expanding the left side gives

240 − 5d + 10d = 360,

which simplifies to

240 + 5d = 360.

Subtracting 240 from both sides yields

5d = 120,

and dividing both sides by 5 gives

d = 24.

Thus the bank contains 24 dimes. As a check, this leaves 48 − 24 = 24 nickels, and the total value is 24(10) + 24(5) = 240 + 120 = 360 cents, or $3.60, as required.

The correct answer is D.

## Q17
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Venn Diagrams

In a class of 80 students, 45 study Spanish, 38 study French, and 12 study neither language. How many students study both Spanish and French?

- A) 9
- B) 12
- C) 15
- D) 18
- E) 23

**answer:** C
**explanation:** This problem concerns two overlapping groups within a finite population, so the governing relationship is the inclusion-exclusion principle: for any two sets, the number of elements belonging to at least one of the sets equals the sum of the sizes of the two sets minus the number of elements belonging to both. We must account separately for the members of the population who belong to neither set.

Let the total number of students be 80. Of these, 12 study neither language, so the number of students who study at least one of the two languages is the total less those who study neither:

80 - 12 = 68.

Now let x denote the number of students who study both Spanish and French. The 68 students who study at least one language are exactly the students in the union of the Spanish group and the French group. By the inclusion-exclusion principle,

(number studying Spanish) + (number studying French) - (number studying both) = (number studying at least one),

which translates into

45 + 38 - x = 68.

The left side simplifies to

83 - x = 68.

Solving for x, we subtract 68 from 83:

x = 83 - 68 = 15.

Thus 15 students study both Spanish and French.

The correct answer is C.

## Q18
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Digit Problems

A two-digit positive integer N has tens digit t and units digit u. What is the value of N?

(1) t + u = 11
(2) The integer formed by reversing the digits of N is 27 greater than N.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**explanation:** The governing principle is that a two-digit positive integer with tens digit t and units digit u has the value N = 10t + u, where t is an integer from 1 through 9 and u is an integer from 0 through 9. Because there are two unknown digits, two independent equations are required to determine N uniquely.

We evaluate each statement against this principle.

Statement (1) gives t + u = 11. This single equation in two unknowns is satisfied by several digit pairs, for example t = 2 and u = 9, t = 3 and u = 8, and t = 4 and u = 7, which produce N = 29, N = 38, and N = 47, respectively. Since more than one value of N is possible, statement (1) alone is not sufficient.

Statement (2) states that the integer formed by reversing the digits, namely 10u + t, exceeds N by 27. We translate this into (10u + t) - (10t + u) = 27. Simplifying gives 9u - 9t = 27, so 9(u - t) = 27, and therefore u - t = 3. This single equation is again satisfied by several digit pairs, for example t = 1 and u = 4, t = 2 and u = 5, and t = 4 and u = 7, which produce N = 14, N = 25, and N = 47, respectively. Since more than one value of N is possible, statement (2) alone is not sufficient.

Taking the two statements together, we have the system t + u = 11 and u - t = 3. Adding the two equations yields 2u = 14, so u = 7, and substituting back gives t = 4. Therefore N = 10(4) + 7 = 47, a single determined value. Both statements together are sufficient, while neither alone is sufficient.

The correct answer is C.

## Q19
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Venn Diagrams

Of 150 guests at a dinner party, 90 ordered appetizers, 95 ordered dessert, and 70 ordered coffee. If 30 guests ordered all three items and every guest ordered at least one item, how many guests ordered exactly two of the three items?

- A) 15
- B) 25
- C) 35
- D) 45
- E) 55

**answer:** D
**explanation:** This problem is governed by the counting principle for three overlapping sets: when the sizes of three sets are summed, each guest who ordered exactly one item is counted once, each guest who ordered exactly two items is counted twice, and each guest who ordered all three items is counted three times.

Let x = the number of guests who ordered exactly two of the three items, and let y = the number of guests who ordered exactly one item. The number who ordered all three items is given as 30.

Because every one of the 150 guests ordered at least one item, the three groups (exactly one, exactly two, all three) account for all guests:

y + x + 30 = 150,

so y + x = 120.

Next, the sum of the three order totals counts each guest according to how many items that guest ordered. Adding the totals gives 90 + 95 + 70 = 255. Translating the counting principle into an equation:

y + 2x + 3(30) = 255,

y + 2x + 90 = 255,

y + 2x = 165.

Subtracting the first equation from this one eliminates y:

(y + 2x) - (y + x) = 165 - 120,

x = 45.

Thus 45 guests ordered exactly two of the three items.

The correct answer is D.

## Q20
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Cost/Revenue

A company sells widgets for $p each. The company's fixed monthly costs are $F, and the variable cost per widget is $v. In a given month, did the company earn a profit?

(1) The company sold 500 widgets and p - v = $8.
(2) F = $3,000.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**explanation:** This is a data sufficiency question, so the task is to determine whether each statement provides enough information to answer the question "Did the company earn a profit?" with a definite yes or no, rather than to compute a single value. The governing relationship is that profit equals total revenue minus total cost. Let n represent the number of widgets sold in the month. Total revenue is np, the variable cost is nv, and the fixed cost is F, so total cost is F + nv. Translating the words into math, profit = np - (F + nv) = n(p - v) - F. The quantity (p - v) is the contribution margin per widget, and n(p - v) is the total contribution, from which the fixed cost F must be subtracted. Thus the answer depends on three pieces of information: the number sold, the per-unit margin, and the fixed cost.

Consider statement (1). It gives n = 500 and p - v = 8, so the total contribution is 500 times 8, which equals 4,000. However, the fixed cost F is unknown. If F = 2,000, then profit = 4,000 - 2,000 = 2,000, which is positive, so the answer would be yes. If instead F = 10,000, then profit = 4,000 - 10,000 = -6,000, which is negative, so the answer would be no. Because both a yes and a no are possible, statement (1) alone is not sufficient.

Consider statement (2). It gives F = 3,000 but provides no information about the number of widgets sold or the per-unit margin, so the total contribution n(p - v) is unknown. The contribution could be any nonnegative amount, making profit either positive or negative. Therefore statement (2) alone is not sufficient.

Now consider the two statements together. From statement (1), the total contribution is n(p - v) = 4,000, and from statement (2), the fixed cost is F = 3,000. Substituting into profit = n(p - v) - F gives profit = 4,000 - 3,000 = 1,000. Since 1,000 is greater than 0, the company earned a profit, and the answer is a definite yes. The two statements together are sufficient, while neither statement alone is sufficient.

The correct answer is C.

**explanation:** This problem is solved by recognizing that profit is a piecewise linear function of the quantity produced, and that a piecewise linear function attains its maximum at a boundary or at the point where the rule changes. The governing method is therefore to express profit separately on each segment, determine the direction in which profit moves on each segment, and compare the candidate values.

Let n denote the number of gadgets produced and sold in a day, with 40 ≤ n ≤ 80. Each gadget costs $10 in materials, and fixed operating costs are $300 per day, so total daily cost is 10n + 300.

P = 25n − (10n + 300) = 15n − 300.

P = 15(50) − 300 = 750 − 300 = 450.

25(50) + 8(n − 50) = 1250 + 8n − 400 = 850 + 8n.

**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Consecutive Even Integers

The sum of four consecutive even integers is 100. What is the product of the smallest and the largest of these integers?

- A) 576
- B) 600
- C) 616
- D) 648
- E) 672

**answer:** C
**explanation:** This problem is solved by representing a set of consecutive even integers algebraically and translating the stated sum into a single linear equation. Consecutive even integers differ by 2, so once the smallest is named, each successive integer can be written in terms of it.

Let n represent the smallest of the four integers. The four consecutive even integers are then n, n + 2, n + 4, and n + 6.

The condition that their sum is 100 gives the equation

n + (n + 2) + (n + 4) + (n + 6) = 100.

Combining like terms yields

4n + 12 = 100.

Subtracting 12 from both sides gives

4n = 88,

and dividing both sides by 4 gives

n = 22.

The four integers are therefore 22, 24, 26, and 28. The smallest is 22 and the largest is 28, so their product is

22 x 28 = 616.

The correct answer is C.

## Q58

**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Compound Interest

An investment earns interest at a fixed compound annual rate. What is the value of the investment after exactly 2 years?

(1) The initial investment is $10,000.
(2) After 1 year, the investment has grown to $11,200.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**explanation:** The value of a fixed-rate compound-interest investment after exactly 2 years is given by P(1 + r)², where P is the principal and r is the annual rate. Determining this value therefore requires knowing both P and r, or equivalently obtaining two independent equations that together fix both quantities.

Let P represent the principal and let r represent the annual compound rate. The quantity sought is P(1 + r)².

Consider statement (1). We are given that P = 10,000, so the target expression becomes 10,000(1 + r)². The rate r remains unknown, and different values of r produce different 2-year values. With one unknown unconstrained, the value cannot be determined. Statement (1) alone is not sufficient.

Consider statement (2). We are given that the value after 1 year is 11,200, that is, P(1 + r) = 11,200. This is a single equation in the two unknowns P and r. For instance, P = 10,000 with r = 0.12 satisfies it, and so does P = 11,200/1.05 with r = 0.05; these cases yield different 2-year values. Statement (2) alone is not sufficient.

Consider the statements together. From statement (1) we have P = 10,000, and substituting into statement (2) gives 10,000(1 + r) = 11,200. Dividing both sides by 10,000 yields 1 + r = 1.12, so r = 0.12. The 2-year value is then 10,000(1.12)² = 10,000 × 1.2544 = 12,544. Both unknowns are determined, so the value is determined. The two statements together are sufficient.

The correct answer is C.

## Q59

**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Weighted Average — Find Proportion

In a class, the boys' average score on a test is 78 and the girls' average score is 84. The overall class average is 80. What fraction of the class consists of boys?

- A) 1/4
- B) 1/3
- C) 1/2
- D) 3/5
- E) 2/3

**answer:** E
**explanation:** This is a weighted-average problem in which the overall class average is a weighted mean of the two group averages, with the weights equal to each group's fraction of the class. The governing relationship is that the overall average equals the boys' average times the boys' fraction plus the girls' average times the girls' fraction.

Let b be the fraction of the class that consists of boys. Because every student is either a boy or a girl, the fraction that consists of girls is 1 minus b.

Translating the given averages into the weighted-average equation gives the following.

78b + 84(1 - b) = 80

We expand the left side.

78b + 84 - 84b = 80

We combine the b terms.

-6b + 84 = 80

We subtract 84 from both sides.

-6b = -4

We divide both sides by -6.

b = 4/6 = 2/3

Thus the fraction of the class that consists of boys is 2/3.

The correct answer is E.

## Q60

**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Investment — Two Accounts

Alec invested a total of $15,000 across two accounts, P and Q. What was the total interest Alec earned from both accounts after one year?

(1) Account P pays 4% annual simple interest and Account Q pays 6% annual simple interest.
(2) Alec invested twice as much in Account Q as in Account P.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**explanation:** The simple interest earned on an account over one year equals the product of the principal invested and the annual interest rate, and the total interest is the sum of the interest earned on each account. Determining the total interest therefore requires knowing both the rate and the principal for each of the two accounts.

Let x = the amount invested in Account P. Then the amount invested in Account Q is 15,000 - x, since the two accounts together hold a total of $15,000.

Statement (1) provides the two rates: Account P pays 4% and Account Q pays 6%. The total interest is then 0.04x + 0.06(15,000 - x) = 0.04x + 900 - 0.06x = 900 - 0.02x. Because the value of x is not fixed, this expression takes different values for different allocations. For example, if x = 5,000, the total interest is 900 - 0.02(5,000) = 800; if x = 7,500, the total interest is 900 - 0.02(7,500) = 750. The rates alone do not determine the total interest; statement (1) is not sufficient.

Statement (2) provides the relationship between the two principals: the amount in Account Q is twice the amount in Account P, so 15,000 - x = 2x. Solving, 15,000 = 3x, which gives x = 5,000, and therefore Account P holds $5,000 and Account Q holds $10,000. However, no interest rates are given, so the interest earned cannot be computed. Statement (2) is not sufficient.

Taking the two statements together, we know both the principals and the rates. Account P earns 0.04 × 5,000 = 200, and Account Q earns 0.06 × 10,000 = 600. The total interest is 200 + 600 = 800, a single determined value. The two statements together are sufficient, but neither statement alone is sufficient.

The correct answer is C.

## Q61

**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Work Rate — Phase Change

A construction team begins work on a pipeline at a constant daily rate. After completing exactly 3/8 of the pipeline, the team acquires new equipment that doubles their daily construction rate. The entire pipeline is completed in 22 days total. How many days did construction take at the original (slower) rate?

- A) 6
- B) 8
- C) 10
- D) 12
- E) 16

**answer:** D
**explanation:** In a problem involving a single rate that changes part-way through a task, the governing relationship is that the time spent in each phase equals the work completed in that phase divided by the rate at which it is done, and the sum of the phase times equals the total elapsed time.

Let r denote the team's original construction rate, measured as the fraction of the pipeline completed per day. In the first phase the team completes 3/8 of the pipeline at rate r, so the time required is t1 = (3/8) / r. After acquiring the new equipment the rate doubles to 2r, and the team completes the remaining 5/8 of the pipeline, so the time required is t2 = (5/8) / (2r) = 5 / (16r).

The entire pipeline is completed in 22 days, which gives the equation t1 + t2 = 22. We translate this into

(3 / (8r)) + (5 / (16r)) = 22.

Expressing both terms over a common denominator of 16r, we obtain

(6 / (16r)) + (5 / (16r)) = (11 / (16r)) = 22.

Solving for r, we have 11 = 22 · 16r, so r = 11 / (22 · 16) = 1 / 32. Thus the original rate is 1/32 of the pipeline per day.

The number of days at the original rate is t1 = (3/8) / (1/32) = (3/8) · 32 = 12.

As a check, in 12 days at a rate of 1/32 per day the team completes 12/32 = 3/8 of the pipeline, and the remaining 22 - 12 = 10 days at the doubled rate of 1/16 per day complete 10/16 = 5/8 of the pipeline, accounting for the whole job.

The correct answer is D.

## Q62

**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Multi-Constraint Payroll

A landscaping crew works a 5-hour job at an estate. The crew consists of gardeners paid $40 per hour and assistants paid $20 per hour. The number of assistants is exactly 4 more than the number of gardeners. At the end of the job, the total wages paid to gardeners exceeded the total wages paid to assistants by $400. How many workers are on the crew in total?

- A) 12
- B) 14
- C) 16
- D) 18
- E) 20

**answer:** E
**explanation:** This problem is solved by translating each stated constraint into an algebraic equation and then solving for the requested quantity. Because the unknowns are linked, we express everything in terms of a single variable.

Let g represent the number of gardeners. Since the number of assistants is exactly 4 more than the number of gardeners, the number of assistants is g + 4.

Each worker is paid for the full 5-hour job. Gardeners earn $40 per hour, so the total wages paid to gardeners equal 40 multiplied by 5 multiplied by g, which is 200g. Assistants earn $20 per hour, so the total wages paid to assistants equal 20 multiplied by 5 multiplied by (g + 4), which is 100(g + 4), or 100g + 400.

The condition that the gardeners' total wages exceeded the assistants' total wages by $400 translates into the equation 200g minus (100g + 400) equals 400. Simplifying the left side gives 100g minus 400 equals 400. Adding 400 to both sides gives 100g equals 800, so g equals 8.

Thus there are 8 gardeners, and the number of assistants is 8 + 4, which is 12. The question asks for the total number of workers on the crew, which is 8 + 12, or 20.

The correct answer is E.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-05-word-problems
**takeaway:** Multi-constraint word problems: translate each constraint into an equation, solve the system for the variable, then complete the final calculation the question actually asks for (total workers = g + (g+4), not just g). Always re-read the final question before writing the answer.
