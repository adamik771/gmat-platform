## Q1
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Rate/Time/Distance

A car travels at 60 miles per hour for 2.5 hours. How many miles does it travel?

- A) 120
- B) 130
- C) 140
- D) 150
- E) 160

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** D = R·T = 60 × 2.5 = 150.
**explanation:** D = R × T = 60 × 2.5 = 150 mi. Decompose 2.5 hrs: 60 × 2 = 120 for the first 2 hours, plus 60 × 0.5 = 30 for the half-hour: 150.
**mistake_a:** Multiplied 60 × 2 = 120 only — handled the whole hours correctly but forgot to add the half-hour distance (60 × 0.5 = 30 additional miles).
**mistake_b:** Treated the half-hour as one-sixth of 60 (10 miles) rather than one-half (30 miles): 120 + 10 = 130. The error comes from confusing "0.5 hr" with "1/6 hr."
**mistake_c:** Treated the half-hour as one-third of 60 (20 miles) rather than one-half: 120 + 20 = 140. Equivalently, mistook 30 minutes for 20 minutes worth of distance.
**mistake_e:** Treated the half-hour as two-thirds of 60 (40 miles): 120 + 40 = 160. Equivalently, confused "0.5 hours" with "40 minutes" (two-thirds of an hour).
**common_trap:** Ignoring or mis-computing the fractional-hour portion. 0.5 hours = 30 minutes = exactly half the hourly rate.
**takeaway:** D = R × T. For fractional hours, decompose: 60 × 2 + 60 × 0.5 = 120 + 30 = 150. Half of 60 is 30, not 10 or 20.

## Q2
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Profit/Loss

A merchant buys a product for $40 and sells it for $52. What is the merchant's profit as a percentage of the cost?

- A) 12%
- B) 20%
- C) 23%
- D) 25%
- E) 30%

**answer:** E
**fastest_path:** Profit/cost = 12/40 = 30%.
**explanation:** Profit = 52 − 40 = 12. Profit % of cost = (12/40) × 100 = 30%.
**mistake_a:** Bubbled the dollar profit (12) as a percent — stated "12%" without dividing by the cost.
**mistake_b:** Estimated 20% without computing: 20% of 40 = 8 ≠ 12.
**mistake_c:** Computed profit as a percentage of the *selling price* instead of cost: 12/52 ≈ 23%. The question specifies "percentage of cost," so the base must be 40, not 52.
**mistake_d:** Computed 12/48 = 25% (used an incorrect cost of $48, perhaps from rounding $52 to a nearby number).
**common_trap:** Computing profit as % of *selling price* (12/52 ≈ 23%) instead of % of *cost* (12/40 = 30%). Always identify the base.
**takeaway:** Profit % of cost = (profit ÷ cost) × 100. Profit % of selling price = (profit ÷ SP) × 100. These give different answers — always identify the base the question specifies.

## Q3
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Age Problems

Sarah is 4 times as old as her brother David. If Sarah is 28 years old, how old will David be in 5 years?

- A) 7
- B) 9
- C) 10
- D) 12
- E) 14

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** David now = 28/4 = 7. In 5 years: 7 + 5 = 12.
**explanation:** Translate: Sarah = 4 × David → 28 = 4D → D = 7. The question asks for David's age in 5 years: 7 + 5 = 12. Two steps: find the current age, then apply the time shift.
**mistake_a:** Computed David's current age (28/4 = 7) and bubbled it. The question asks for his age *in 5 years*, not now. Always re-read what time the question asks about.
**mistake_b:** Applied a 2-year offset instead of 5: 7 + 2 = 9. Misread "in 5 years" as "in 2 years."
**mistake_c:** Misread "Sarah is 4 times as old" as "Sarah is 4 times *more than* David," meaning Sarah = 5 × David. Computed David = 28/5 ≈ 5.6, then added 5 years ≈ 10.6, rounded to 10. "A is k times as old as B" means A = k × B (not A = B + k·B).
**mistake_e:** Doubled David's current age: 2 × 7 = 14. Confused "in 5 years" with "if the ratio doubled."
**common_trap:** Bubbling the current age (7) instead of the future age, or misinterpreting "4 times as old" as "4 times more than."
**takeaway:** Two-step age problems: (1) find current ages using the given ratio or equation, (2) apply the time shift. "A is k times as old as B" means A = k·B.

## Q4
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Work Rate

A printer can print 240 pages in 8 minutes. At this rate, how many pages can it print in 15 minutes?

- A) 360
- B) 400
- C) 420
- D) 450
- E) 480

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** Rate = 30 ppm. 30 × 15 = 450.
**explanation:** Rate = 240/8 = 30 pages/min. In 15 min: 30 × 15 = 450.
**mistake_a:** Got rate = 30 ppm correctly, but used 12 minutes instead of 15: 30 × 12 = 360. A common slip when writing down the target time.
**mistake_b:** Set up the proportion as 240 × (15/9) = 400 — used 9 in the denominator instead of 8, perhaps from miscounting the given interval by one: (15/9) × 240 = 400.
**mistake_c:** Got rate = 30 ppm correctly, but computed 30 × 14 = 420 — used 14 minutes instead of 15. Off-by-one on the target time.
**mistake_e:** Got rate = 30 ppm correctly, but computed 30 × 16 = 480 — added 1 to the given time (treating "15 minutes" as "16 cycles" or similar off-by-one).
**common_trap:** Correct rate, wrong time — slipping on the 15-minute multiplier. Work the two steps separately: rate = given/given_time, then output = rate × new_time.
**takeaway:** Two-step rate problems: rate = output/time, then new output = rate × new time. Write the rate down explicitly before multiplying by the new time.

## Q5
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Rate/Time/Distance

Train A leaves Station X heading east at 70 mph. Two hours later, Train B leaves Station X heading east at 90 mph. How many hours after Train B departs will it overtake Train A?

- A) 5
- B) 6
- C) 7
- D) 8
- E) 9

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Head start = 140 mi. Closing rate = 20 mph. T = 140/20 = 7 hr.
**explanation:** When B departs, A has gone 70 × 2 = 140 mi ahead. Since both travel the same direction, B closes the gap at (90 − 70) = 20 mph. Time = 140/20 = 7 hr.
**mistake_a:** Computed head-start distance as 2 × 50 = 100 mi (used the average of the two speeds, 80/2 = 50, instead of Train A's actual speed of 70): 100/20 = 5 hr.
**mistake_b:** Computed head-start distance using 60 mph instead of 70 mph for Train A — perhaps misread the problem: 60 × 2 = 120 mi; then 120/20 = 6 hr.
**mistake_d:** Computed head-start distance using the average of both speeds (70 + 90)/2 = 80 mph: 80 × 2 = 160 mi; then 160/20 = 8 hr.
**mistake_e:** Used the *sum* of speeds as the closing rate instead of the *difference*: 140/(90 + 70) = 140/160 ≈ 0.875 hr → noticed this was wrong and flipped to 140/16 ≈ 9. (Summing speeds applies when objects approach from opposite directions, not when chasing from behind.)
**common_trap:** Using sum of speeds (for head-on approach) instead of difference of speeds (for same-direction catch-up). Always: same direction → closing rate = difference; opposite directions → closing rate = sum.
**takeaway:** Catch-up problems: head-start distance ÷ (faster speed − slower speed) = time for faster to catch up. Compute the head-start distance first, then divide by the speed *difference*.

## Q6
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Work Rate

Machine A can complete a job in 6 hours. Machine B can complete the same job in 12 hours. If both machines work together, how many hours will it take to complete the job?

- A) 3
- B) 4
- C) 5
- D) 6
- E) 9

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** 1/6 + 1/12 = 1/4 → T = 4 hr.
**explanation:** Combined rate = 1/6 + 1/12 = 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4 job/hr → T = 4 hr. Or use T = ab/(a+b) = 72/18 = 4.
**mistake_a:** Misread Machine B's time as 6 hours (same as A) instead of 12: combined rate = 1/6 + 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3 → T = 3 hr. The error is taking B's rate from a misread of the problem.
**mistake_c:** Used an average-based estimate: average of the two times = (6 + 4)/2 = 5, or computed combined rate with a fraction arithmetic error yielding 1/5 → T = 5. Times do not average — *rates* add.
**mistake_d:** Bubbled Machine A's solo time (6 hours) — correctly combined rates but reported the input rather than the output of the calculation.
**mistake_e:** Averaged the two machine times: (6 + 12)/2 = 9. This is the most tempting wrong path: it feels intuitive but is always incorrect for concurrent workers.
**common_trap:** Averaging solo times (9) instead of adding rates (1/4 → T = 4). Rates add; times do not.
**takeaway:** For concurrent workers: (1) convert each time to a rate (1/time), (2) add rates, (3) take reciprocal for combined time. Formula: T = ab/(a+b). Never average times.

## Q7
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixture

A chemist has 10 liters of a 30% acid solution. How many liters of pure acid must be added to create a 50% acid solution?

- A) 2
- B) 3
- C) 4
- D) 5
- E) 6

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Acid = 3 L. (3+x)/(10+x) = 0.5 → x = 4.
**explanation:** Initial acid = 0.30 × 10 = 3 L. After adding x L pure acid: (3+x)/(10+x) = 0.5 → 3 + x = 5 + 0.5x → 0.5x = 2 → x = 4.
**mistake_a:** Used the *original* total volume in the denominator (not the growing total): (3 + x)/10 = 0.5 → 3 + x = 5 → x = 2. Forgot that adding x liters of acid also increases the total volume from 10 to (10 + x).
**mistake_b:** Applied the current concentration as the amount to add: "30% of 10 = 3, so add 3 liters." This confuses the current concentration with the amount of acid needed — adding 3 L gives (6/13) ≈ 46%, not 50%.
**mistake_d:** Computed 50% of the original 10 liters = 5 and concluded "add 5 liters of acid." This treats the target concentration as a fraction of the original volume, not the new (10 + x) volume. Result: (8/15) ≈ 53%, not 50%.
**mistake_e:** Set up the ratio as acid/added = target: 3/x = 0.5 → x = 6. This compares existing acid to added acid, not acid to total solution. The correct setup is (acid + added) / (total + added) = 0.5.
**common_trap:** Fixing the denominator at 10 instead of using (10 + x). Adding pure acid changes *both* the acid amount and the total volume.
**takeaway:** When adding a pure substance: new concentration = (original amount + added) / (original volume + added). Both numerator and denominator grow by x.

## Q8
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Profit/Loss

A retailer marks up the cost of goods by 40% and then offers a seasonal discount of 10% on the marked price. What is the retailer's profit as a percentage of cost?

- A) 20%
- B) 24%
- C) 26%
- D) 30%
- E) 36%

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 1.40 × 0.90 = 1.26 → 26% profit on cost.
**explanation:** Set cost = $100. Marked = 100 × 1.40 = $140. After 10% off: 140 × 0.90 = $126. Profit = $26 → 26%.
**mistake_a:** Used additive logic: 40% − 10% − (some adjustment) = 20%. Percent changes applied to different bases cannot be added or subtracted.
**mistake_b:** Computed 1.40 × 0.90 but made the arithmetic error of subtracting 0.16 instead of 0.14 (computed 10% of 1.40 as 0.16 rather than 0.14): 1.40 − 0.16 = 1.24 → 24% profit.
**mistake_d:** Used additive percent logic: markup − discount = 40% − 10% = 30%. This ignores that the 10% discount is applied to the *marked price* ($140), not the original cost ($100). A 10% discount off $140 = $14 reduction, not a 10% reduction of the base $100.
**mistake_e:** Multiplied the markup percentage by (1 − discount%): 40% × 0.90 = 36%. This applies the discount to the markup *percentage* rather than to the *price*. Percentages applied to different bases cannot be multiplied this way.
**common_trap:** Treating successive percent changes additively (40 − 10 = 30%) when the 10% discount is applied to the marked-up price, not the original cost.
**takeaway:** Chain multipliers: set cost = $100, apply each factor to the running price. Net = 1.40 × 0.90 = 1.26 → 26% profit. Additive shortcuts (40 − 10 = 30) fail because each percent operates on a different base.

## Q9
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Rate/Time/Distance

A cyclist travels from Town A to Town B at 15 mph and returns by the same route at 10 mph. What is the cyclist's average speed, in miles per hour, for the entire round trip?

- A) 11
- B) 12
- C) 12.5
- D) 13
- E) 13.5

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** Equal distances → harmonic mean: 2(15)(10)/(15+10) = 300/25 = 12.
**explanation:** Equal-distance round trip → harmonic mean: 2ab/(a+b) = 2·15·10/25 = 12 mph. From first principles: let each leg = D. Total time = D/15 + D/10 = 5D/30 = D/6. Average speed = 2D/(D/6) = 12.
**mistake_a:** Used harmonic mean formula but introduced an arithmetic error: added a spurious term to the denominator (e.g., 15 + 10 + 2 = 27 instead of 25), yielding 300/27 ≈ 11.1 ≈ 11.
**mistake_c:** Took the simple arithmetic mean of the two speeds: (15 + 10)/2 = 12.5. This equals average speed only when equal *time* is spent at each speed — but on equal-distance legs, more time is spent at the slower speed (10 mph), pulling the average *below* the arithmetic mean.
**mistake_d:** Weighted incorrectly: (15 × 2/3 + 10 × 1/3) = 10 + 3.3 = 13.3 ≈ 13. Assigned two-thirds weight to the faster leg without justification.
**mistake_e:** Averaged only the faster and the arithmetic-mean values: (15 + 12)/2 = 13.5. The 12 is the correct answer, making this a variant of the arithmetic-mean error.
**common_trap:** Using the arithmetic mean (12.5) for equal-distance trips. Equal-distance trips weight the slower leg more heavily in time, so the true average is always *below* the arithmetic mean.
**takeaway:** Equal distance both ways → harmonic mean: 2ab/(a+b). Equal time both ways → arithmetic mean: (a+b)/2. Identify which quantity is equal before choosing the formula.

## Q10
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Work Rate

Pipe A can fill a tank in 4 hours, and Pipe B can fill the same tank in 6 hours. Pipe C can drain the full tank in 12 hours. If all three pipes are open simultaneously, how many hours will it take to fill the empty tank?

- A) 2
- B) 3
- C) 4
- D) 5
- E) 6

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** Net rate = 1/4 + 1/6 − 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3 → T = 3.
**explanation:** Net rate = 1/4 + 1/6 − 1/12 = 3/12 + 2/12 − 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3 → T = 3 hr.
**mistake_a:** Treated Pipe C as an additional *fill* pipe instead of a drain, adding all three rates: 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 2/12 + 1/12 = 6/12 = 1/2 → T = 2 hr.
**mistake_c:** Ignored Pipes B and C entirely and used only Pipe A's rate: 1/4 → T = 4 hr. Or: combined only Pipe A with the drain (forgetting B): 1/4 − 1/12 = 2/12 = 1/6 → T = 6 (which is E).
**mistake_d:** Combined fill rates correctly (1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12) but then made a fraction arithmetic error subtracting the drain: computed 5/12 − 1/12 = 4/12 = 1/3 correctly in principle but obtained 1/5 through a denominator slip → T = 5.
**mistake_e:** Forgot to subtract the drain and computed only the two fill pipes: 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12 → T = 12/5 ≈ 2.4. Or combined only Pipe A with the drain: 1/4 − 1/12 = 2/12 = 1/6 → T = 6.
**common_trap:** Forgetting the *negative* sign on the drain rate — treating Pipe C as a fill pipe and adding instead of subtracting its rate.
**takeaway:** Drains have *negative* rates. Net = Σ fill rates − Σ drain rates. Always label each pipe's sign before computing.

## Q11
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixture

A grocer mixes Brand A coffee at $12 per pound with Brand B coffee at $8 per pound to create a blend that sells at $9.50 per pound. If the grocer makes 40 pounds of the blend, how many pounds of Brand A coffee are used?

- A) 10
- B) 12
- C) 15
- D) 18
- E) 20

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Alligation: $9.50 is 1.50 from $8 and 2.50 from $12. So A:B = 1.5:2.5 = 3:5. A = (3/8)·40 = 15.
**explanation:** Let a + b = 40 and 12a + 8b = 380. Substitute b = 40 − a: 12a + 320 − 8a = 380 → 4a = 60 → a = 15. Alligation check: blend $9.50 is $1.50 above cheapest and $2.50 below priciest → A:B = 1.5:2.5 = 3:5 → A = 3/8 × 40 = 15.
**mistake_a:** Used $9.00 as the blend price instead of $9.50: 12a + 8(40−a) = 360 → 4a = 40 → a = 10. A one-dollar misread of the blend price propagates directly into the answer.
**mistake_b:** Used $9.25 as the blend price: 12a + 8(40−a) = 370 → 4a = 50 → a = 12.5, rounded to 12. Another price misread — reading the decimal incorrectly.
**mistake_d:** Applied alligation with un-inverted ratio: used A:B = 2.50:1.50 = 5:3 (did not invert; assigned A's amount to A's own distance from the blend rather than B's distance) → A = 5/8 × 40 = 25. Then misapplied further to get 18 through arithmetic error on 5/8 × 40.
**mistake_e:** Took half of the total: 40/2 = 20, assuming Brand A and Brand B contribute equally. The blend price ($9.50) is closer to $8 than to $12, so Brand B (cheaper) dominates.
**common_trap:** In alligation, each ingredient's *quantity* corresponds to the *other* ingredient's distance from the blend price — the ratio is inverted. Brand A's pounds correspond to $9.50 − $8.00 = $1.50, not to $12 − $9.50 = $2.50.
**takeaway:** Alligation ratio: A quantity : B quantity = (blend − B price) : (A price − blend). The quantities are inversely proportional to the distances. Verify with algebra: check that 12a + 8b equals 9.50 × 40.

## Q12
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Age Problems

Five years ago, James was 4 times as old as his nephew. In 3 years, James will be twice as old as his nephew. How old is James now?

- A) 21
- B) 25
- C) 29
- D) 33
- E) 37

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** J = 4N − 15; J = 2N + 3 → 2N = 18 → N = 9 → J = 21.
**explanation:** J − 5 = 4(N − 5) → J = 4N − 15. J + 3 = 2(N + 3) → J = 2N + 3. Equate: 2N = 18 → N = 9 → J = 21. Verify: 5 yrs ago 16 = 4·4 ✓; in 3 yrs 24 = 2·12 ✓.
**mistake_b:** Applied the time shift to James but not to the nephew in the "5 years ago" equation: wrote J − 5 = 4N (forgot to also subtract 5 from N) → J = 4N − 5. Combined with J = 2N + 3: 4N − 5 = 2N + 3 → N = 4 → J = 11... or with a second translation error lands near 25.
**mistake_c:** Applied the time shift to the nephew but not to James in the "3 years from now" equation: wrote J = 2(N + 3) − 3 or some variant, shifting only one person's age, yielding J ≈ 29.
**mistake_d:** Set up both equations with wrong signs: wrote J + 5 = 4(N + 5) and J − 3 = 2(N − 3) (added instead of subtracted for "5 years ago" and vice versa) → over-estimated James's current age to ≈ 33.
**mistake_e:** Interpreted "4 times as old" as "4 years older" rather than "4× the age": J − 5 = N − 5 + 4 → J = N + 4. With J + 3 = 2(N + 3): N + 4 + 3 = 2N + 6 → N = 1 → J = 5... then overcorrected to reach 37.
**common_trap:** Applying the time shift to only one person. "5 years ago" means *both* James and his nephew are 5 years younger: both ages are reduced by 5.
**takeaway:** For age problems with time shifts: every person's age changes by the same offset. "N years ago" → subtract N from every person's current age in that equation. "N years from now" → add N to every person's age.

The sum of three consecutive integers is 72. What is the largest of the three integers?

**common_trap:** Bubbling the mean (24) instead of the largest. For consecutive integers with odd count: mean = middle; largest = middle + (count − 1)/2.
**takeaway:** Consecutive integers: middle = sum/count. Largest = middle + (count − 1)/2. For 3 integers: largest = middle + 1.

## Q14
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Averages

The average (arithmetic mean) of 5 numbers is 18. If a sixth number is added and the new average is 20, what is the sixth number?

- A) 22
- B) 24
- C) 28
- D) 30
- E) 32

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** New sum 6·20 = 120. Old sum 5·18 = 90. 6th = 30.
**explanation:** Old sum = 90; new sum = 120. 6th = 120 − 90 = 30. Shortcut: new value = new mean + n × Δmean = 20 + 5 × 2 = 30.
**mistake_a:** Computed the difference of averages and multiplied by the wrong count: (20 − 18) × (5 − 4) = 2, then added to 20: 20 + 2 = 22. Worked in means rather than sums.
**mistake_b:** Computed 20 + (20 − 18) × 2 = 24 from a misapplied adjustment formula — used 2 as a factor when the correct factor is 5 (the original count).
**mistake_c:** Computed new mean × original count: 20 × (6 − 5) + 18 = 28 via arithmetic confusion.
**mistake_e:** Added both averages: 18 + 20 − (20 − 18) = 36... or computed 20 + 18 − 6 = 32. Used some combination of the given numbers without the correct sum-difference logic.
**common_trap:** Working with means instead of sums. The difference of means (20 − 18 = 2) tells you each of the original 5 numbers needs to "contribute" 2 more — the 6th number must cover all that extra: 5 × 2 = 10 extra, plus the new mean of 20.
**takeaway:** For mean problems, always work in *sums*. Sum = mean × count. New value = new sum − old sum = 6·20 − 5·18 = 120 − 90 = 30.

## Q15

**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Age Problems

Ten years ago, Sara was twice as old as her brother Tom. Today, the sum of their ages is 62. How old is Sara now?

- A) 32
- B) 36
- C) 38
- D) 40
- E) 42

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** S − 10 = 2(T − 10) → S = 2T − 10. With S + T = 62 → T = 24, S = 38.
**explanation:** S − 10 = 2(T − 10) → S = 2T − 10. Substitute into S + T = 62: 3T − 10 = 62 → T = 24 → S = 38.
**mistake_a:** Wrote the "10 years ago" equation without applying the shift to Tom: S − 10 = 2T (shifted only Sara's age) → S = 2T + 10. With S + T = 62: 3T + 10 = 62 → T = 52/3 ≈ 17, S ≈ 44... arithmetic confusion leads to 32.
**mistake_b:** Solved S + T = 62 for S = 2T (using only the ratio from the stated condition without the time shift): 3T = 62 → T ≈ 20.7, S ≈ 41.3. Then adjusted incorrectly toward 36.
**mistake_d:** Used the equation S = 2T (ignoring the "10 years ago" offset entirely): 2T + T = 62 → T ≈ 20.7, S ≈ 41.3 ≈ 40 after rounding. The "10 years ago" framing is dropped.
**mistake_e:** Applied the time shift in the wrong direction: S + 10 = 2(T + 10) → S = 2T + 10. With S + T = 62: 3T + 10 = 62 → T = 17.3, S = 44.7 ≈ 42 after rounding.
**common_trap:** Shifting only Sara's age and not Tom's — the "10 years ago" applies to both people. Both ages are 10 less than they are today.
**takeaway:** "N years ago" applies to every person in the equation. Always subtract N from each person's current age when setting up the historical equation.

## Q16
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Coin Problems

A piggy bank contains only nickels and dimes. If the bank contains 48 coins worth $3.60 in total, how many dimes are in the bank?

- A) 18
- B) 20
- C) 22
- D) 24
- E) 28

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** n + d = 48; 5n + 10d = 360 (cents) → d = 24.
**explanation:** n + d = 48 and 5n + 10d = 360. Substitute n = 48 − d: 5(48 − d) + 10d = 360 → 240 + 5d = 360 → d = 24.
