## Q1
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

What is 35% of 80?

- A) 24
- B) 26
- C) 28
- D) 32
- E) 35

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 10% of 80 = 8 → 30% = 24, +5% = 4 → 28.
**explanation:** 35% of 80 = 0.35 × 80 = 28. Faster: 30% (24) + 5% (4) = 28.
**mistake_a:** Computed 30% only (24); forgot the +5%.
**mistake_b:** Slip → 26.
**mistake_d:** Computed 40% (32); rounded up.
**mistake_e:** Bubbled the percent value (35) directly.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the percent number itself, or computing only the round 30% portion.
**takeaway:** Break percents into 10% and 5% chunks: 10% = ÷10, 5% = ÷20. Faster than decimals.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q2

**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

If 18 is x percent of 60, what is the value of x?

- A) 18
- B) 24
- C) 30
- D) 36
- E) 42

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 18/60 = 3/10 = 30%.
**explanation:** 18 = (x/100)(60) → x = 1800/60 = 30. Or: 18/60 = 3/10 = 30%.
**mistake_a:** Bubbled the part value (18) directly.
**mistake_b:** Slip → 24.
**mistake_d:** Slip → 36.
**mistake_e:** Slip → 42.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the part (18) instead of computing the percent.
**takeaway:** Memorize fraction ↔ percent: 1/4 = 25%, 3/10 = 30%, 3/8 = 37.5%, 5/8 = 62.5%. Recognition saves time.

## Q3
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Ratios

A box contains only red and green pens in the ratio 4:7. If there are 44 pens in total, how many green pens are in the box?

- A) 16
- B) 22
- C) 24
- D) 28
- E) 32

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** 4 + 7 = 11 parts. 44/11 = 4 per part. Green = 7 × 4 = 28.
**explanation:** Ratio parts sum to 11 → each part = 44/11 = 4. Green = 7 × 4 = 28.
**mistake_a:** Bubbled red count: 4 × 4 = 16.
**mistake_b:** Bubbled half the total (22).
**mistake_c:** Slip → 24.
**mistake_e:** Slip → 32.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the *other* color (red, 16) by mis-reading the question.
**takeaway:** Identify which quantity the question asks for *before* computing. Always confirm before bubbling.

## Q5

**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Proportions

On a map, 2 inches represents 15 actual miles. If two cities are 7 inches apart on the map, how many actual miles apart are they?

- A) 30
- B) 45
- C) 52.5

- E) 105

**mistake_a:** Used 2 × 15 = 30 (mis-read which value scales).
**mistake_b:** Slip → 45.
**mistake_d:** Slip → 60.
**mistake_e:** Forgot to divide by 2: 7 × 15 = 105.
**common_trap:** Forgetting the divisor (2 inches) and multiplying 7 × 15 = 105.
**takeaway:** Cross-multiply with units labeled (inches/inches = miles/miles). Prevents flipping.

In a class of 40 students, 14 are left-handed. What percent of the class is left-handed?

**mistake_a:** Mis-simplified 14/40 → 28% (took 14 × 2 instead of × 2.5).

**mistake_d:** Slip → 38%.
**mistake_e:** Bubbled total size (40%).
**common_trap:** Mis-converting /20 fractions to /100 (× 5, not × 4 or × 6).
**takeaway:** /20 fractions: × 5 to convert to percent. /25: × 4. Memorize denominators that convert cleanly.

## Q7
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Percent Change

The price of a stock increases by 20% in January, then decreases by 20% in February. By what percent has the stock's price changed from the start of January to the end of February?

- A) It decreased by 4%
- B) It decreased by 2%
- C) It is unchanged
- D) It increased by 2%
- E) It increased by 4%

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → −4%.
**explanation:** Successive % changes compound multiplicatively: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → 4% decrease. Smart number 100: 100 → 120 → 96.
**mistake_b:** Mis-computed compound → −2%.
**mistake_c:** Treated as additive (+20 − 20 = 0) → unchanged.
**mistake_d:** Inverted sign → +2%.
**mistake_e:** Inverted sign and rounded → +4%.
**common_trap:** Treating +x% then −x% as canceling. Pattern: net change is always a *loss* of (x/10)²%.
**takeaway:** Successive percent changes compound multiplicatively. +x% then −x% nets a 1 − x²/10000 change — always a loss.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q9
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Profit/Markup/Discount

A retailer marks up a product by 40% over cost, then offers a 25% discount off the marked price. If the cost is $80, what is the retailer's profit on a sale?

- A) $4
- B) $8
- C) $12
- D) $16
- E) $20

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05 → 5% profit on $80 = $4.
**explanation:** Marked = 80 × 1.40 = 112. Sale = 112 × 0.75 = 84. Profit = 84 − 80 = 4. Or chain: 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05 → 5% net profit → $4.
**mistake_b:** Slip → 8.
**mistake_c:** Slip → 12.
**mistake_d:** Computed 20% of 80 = 16 (took percent diff additively).
**mistake_e:** Computed 25% of 80 = 20 (used wrong base for discount).
**common_trap:** Subtracting percents (40 − 25 = 15%) ignores that the 25% discount is taken off the *marked* price, not cost.
**takeaway:** Multipliers chain: net = (1 + markup)(1 − discount). Each percent uses its own base.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-05-word-problems

## Q10
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Ratios

The ratio of a to b is 3:5 and the ratio of b to c is 4:7. What is the ratio of a to c?

- A) 3:7
- B) 12:35
- C) 15:28
- D) 12:28
- E) 21:20

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** Match b. Scale 3:5 → 12:20, scale 4:7 → 20:35. Now a:b:c = 12:20:35 → a:c = 12:35.
**explanation:** Chain ratios by matching b. a:b = 3:5 → 12:20 (×4). b:c = 4:7 → 20:35 (×5). Combined: a:b:c = 12:20:35 → a:c = 12:35.
**mistake_a:** Paired first numerator with last denominator (3:7) — wrong shortcut.
**mistake_c:** Slip → 15:28.
**mistake_d:** Mis-scaled c → 12:28.
**mistake_e:** Inverted ratio → 21:20.
**common_trap:** Pairing first numerator with last denominator (3:7) instead of properly scaling so b matches.
**takeaway:** To chain ratios, the shared term must be *equal* in both. Scale by LCM, then read across.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q11

A company's revenue grew by 50% from 2023 to 2024. If 2024 revenue was $900,000, what was the 2023 revenue?

- A) $450,000

- D) $675,000

**mistake_d:** Slip → 675K.

**common_trap:** Treating "+50% increase" as "half" — divides by 2 instead of by 1.5.

## Q14
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Ratios

In a bag of marbles containing only red and blue marbles, what is the number of red marbles?

(1) The ratio of red marbles to blue marbles is 3:5.
(2) There are 24 more blue marbles than red marbles.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) ALONE is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) ALONE is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** (1) ratio only — infinite scalings. (2) difference only — no ratio. Together: 5k − 3k = 24 → k = 12 → red = 36.
**explanation:** Statement (1): r:b = 3:5, so r = 3k for any k. Insufficient. Statement (2): b − r = 24, but no ratio. Insufficient. Together: 2k = 24 → k = 12 → red = 36. Sufficient.
**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_d:** Each alone — neither is.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient.
**common_trap:** Forgetting that a ratio alone gives infinite valid scalings.
**takeaway:** Ratio + any *one* scalar anchor (total, part, or difference) determines all parts.

## Q16
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixtures

A 50-liter mixture is 40% alcohol. A second mixture, which is 70% alcohol, is added to the first until the resulting mixture is 55% alcohol. How many liters of the second mixture were added?

- A) 25
- B) 40
- C) 50
- D) 60
- E) 75

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Alligation: target 55% is 15 from 40 and 15 from 70 → equal volumes → x = 50.
**explanation:** 0.40(50) + 0.70x = 0.55(50 + x) → 20 + 0.7x = 27.5 + 0.55x → 0.15x = 7.5 → x = 50. Alligation shortcut: 55% is equidistant from 40% and 70% → equal volumes.
**mistake_a:** Slip → 25.
**mistake_b:** Slip → 40.
**mistake_d:** Slip → 60.
**mistake_e:** Slip → 75.
**common_trap:** Setting up the equation with the wrong base on one side, or skipping alligation when distances are equal.
**takeaway:** Alligation: distance ratios = volume ratios (inverted). Equidistant target → equal volumes.

**topic:** Profit/Markup/Discount

A merchant sells a watch for $140 and earns a 30% profit on the selling price. If instead the watch had been sold at a 30% profit on the cost price, what would the selling price have been?

- A) $122.20
- B) $124.60

- D) $127.40

**mistake_a:** Slip → 122.20.
**mistake_b:** Slip → 124.60.
**mistake_c:** Slip → 125.80.
**mistake_e:** Estimated 130 (rough rounding).
**common_trap:** Treating "margin on price" and "markup on cost" as equivalent — they aren't; the *base* differs.
**takeaway:** Always identify the *base* of a percent. "Profit margin" → base is selling price; "markup" → base is cost.

## Q18
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Mixtures

A tank contains a mixture of water and alcohol. What percent of the mixture is alcohol?

(1) If 10 liters of water were added, the mixture would be 20% alcohol.
(2) If 5 liters of alcohol were added, the mixture would be 40% alcohol.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) ALONE is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) ALONE is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Two unknowns (a, t). (1) and (2) each give one equation; together solve a = 7, t = 25 → 28% alcohol.
**explanation:** Let a = alcohol L, t = total L. Statement (1): a/(t+10) = 0.20 — one equation, two unknowns. Insufficient. Statement (2): (a+5)/(t+5) = 0.40 — one equation. Insufficient. Together: solve simultaneously → a = 7, t = 25 → alcohol = 28%. Sufficient.
**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_d:** Each alone — neither is.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient.
**common_trap:** Counting "two facts" without checking that they yield two *independent* equations in the same variables.
**takeaway:** Two unknowns need two independent equations. A "fact" only counts if it adds a new equation, not a restatement.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-05-word-problems
