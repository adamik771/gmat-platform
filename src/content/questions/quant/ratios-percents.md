## Q1
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

What is 35% of 80?

- A) 24
- B) 26
- C) 28
- D) 32
- E) 35

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 10% of 80 = 8 → 30% = 24, +5% = 4 → 28.
**explanation:** 35% of 80 = 0.35 × 80 = 28. Faster: 30% (24) + 5% (4) = 28.
**mistake_a:** Computed 30% only (24); forgot the +5%.
**mistake_b:** Computed only 30% (24) then added 2 instead of 4 for the final 5%: 24 + 2 = 26. The 5% chunk = 10% ÷ 2 = 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
**mistake_d:** Computed 40% (32); rounded up.
**mistake_e:** Bubbled the percent value (35) directly.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the percent number itself, or computing only the round 30% portion.
**takeaway:** Break percents into 10% and 5% chunks: 10% = ÷10, 5% = ÷20. Faster than decimals.

## Q2
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

If 18 is x percent of 60, what is the value of x?

- A) 18
- B) 24
- C) 30
- D) 36
- E) 42

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 18/60 = 3/10 = 30%.
**explanation:** 18 = (x/100)(60) → x = 1800/60 = 30. Or: 18/60 = 3/10 = 30%.
**mistake_a:** Bubbled the part value (18) directly.
**mistake_b:** Multiplied 18/60 by 80 instead of 100: (18/60) × 80 = 24. Always multiply the fraction by 100 to convert to percent, never by another number in the problem.
**mistake_d:** Doubled the part value: 18 × 2 = 36. No valid calculation in this problem produces 36; bubbling a number that looks familiar but has no derivation.
**mistake_e:** Computed the arithmetic difference 60 − 18 = 42, misreading "what percent of 60 is 18" as a subtraction problem.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the part (18) instead of computing the percent.
**takeaway:** Memorize fraction ↔ percent: 1/4 = 25%, 3/10 = 30%, 3/8 = 37.5%, 5/8 = 62.5%. Recognition saves time.

## Q3
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Ratios

A box contains only red and green pens in the ratio 4:7. If there are 44 pens in total, how many green pens are in the box?

- A) 16
- B) 22
- C) 24
- D) 28
- E) 32

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** 4 + 7 = 11 parts. 44/11 = 4 per part. Green = 7 × 4 = 28.
**explanation:** Ratio parts sum to 11 → each part = 44/11 = 4. Green = 7 × 4 = 28.
**mistake_a:** Bubbled red count: 4 × 4 = 16.
**mistake_b:** Bubbled half the total (22).
**mistake_c:** Used 6 parts instead of 7 for green: each part = 44/11 = 4, so 6 × 4 = 24. Off-by-one in the ratio — perhaps started counting from zero or confused part index with count.
**mistake_e:** Counted 8 parts (red + green + 1 phantom) and scaled: 8 × 4 = 32. Always verify the sum of ratio parts: 4 + 7 = 11.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the *other* color (red, 16) by mis-reading the question.
**takeaway:** Identify which quantity the question asks for *before* computing. Always confirm before bubbling.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q4

**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Percent Change

A jacket originally priced at $120 is marked down by 25%. What is the sale price?

- A) $80
- B) $90
- C) $95
- D) $96
- E) $100

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** 0.75 × 120 = 90.
**explanation:** 25% off → pay 75%. 0.75 × 120 = 90. Or: 25% of 120 = 30; 120 − 30 = 90.
**mistake_a:** Computed 1/3 off (mis-translated 25%) → 80.
**mistake_c:** Subtracted a round $25 (instead of computing 25% of $120 = $30): 120 − 25 = 95. Always compute the percent of the base, not just the percent number.
**mistake_d:** Subtracted 20% of $120 = $24 → 120 − 24 = 96, misreading 25% as 20%.
**mistake_e:** Computed 20% off (24 off) → 100, mis-reading the percent.
**common_trap:** Translating 25% as 1/3 (gives 80) instead of 1/4.
**takeaway:** Use multipliers for percent changes: −x% → multiply by (1 − x/100). One-step.

## Q5
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Proportions

On a map, 2 inches represents 15 actual miles. If two cities are 7 inches apart on the map, how many actual miles apart are they?

- A) 30
- B) 45
- C) 52.5
- D) 60
- E) 105

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 7/2 × 15 = 3.5 × 15 = 52.5 mi.
**explanation:** Proportion 2/15 = 7/m → m = 7 × 15/2 = 105/2 = 52.5 mi.
**mistake_a:** Used 2 × 15 = 30 (mis-read which value scales).
**mistake_b:** Rounded 7/2 = 3.5 down to 3 mid-calculation: 3 × 15 = 45. Never round mid-calculation; carry the exact fraction: 3.5 × 15 = 52.5.
**mistake_d:** Misread 7 as 8 (transcription slip) then computed 15/2 × 8 = 60. Re-read the distance from the problem before computing.
**mistake_e:** Forgot to divide by 2: 7 × 15 = 105.
**common_trap:** Forgetting the divisor (2 inches) and multiplying 7 × 15 = 105.
**takeaway:** Cross-multiply with units labeled (inches/inches = miles/miles). Prevents flipping.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q6
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

In a class of 40 students, 14 are left-handed. What percent of the class is left-handed?

- A) 28%
- B) 32%
- C) 35%
- D) 38%
- E) 40%

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 14/40 = 7/20 = 35/100 = 35%.
**explanation:** Percent = part/whole × 100 = 14/40 × 100 = 35%. Or: 14/40 = 7/20, and 7/20 × 5/5 = 35/100 = 35%.
**mistake_a:** Mis-simplified 14/40 → 28% (took 14 × 2 instead of × 2.5).
**mistake_b:** Converted 14/40 by multiplying numerator by 2 instead of 2.5: 14 × 2 = 28... but then used that as a different answer, or: computed 14/40 = 0.32 via arithmetic error (0.40 − 0.08 = 0.32). Cross-check: 0.32 × 40 = 12.8 ≠ 14.
**mistake_d:** Slip to 38%: arithmetic error computing 14/40 × 100 = 38 (perhaps 14 × 100 = 1400, then divided by 37 or made a division error). Verify: 14 × 100 = 1400 ÷ 40 = 35.
**mistake_e:** Bubbled total size (40%).
**common_trap:** Mis-converting /20 fractions to /100 (× 5, not × 4 or × 6).
**takeaway:** /20 fractions: × 5 to convert to percent. /25: × 4. Memorize denominators that convert cleanly.

## Q7
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Percent Change

The price of a stock increases by 20% in January, then decreases by 20% in February. By what percent has the stock's price changed from the start of January to the end of February?

- A) It decreased by 4%
- B) It decreased by 2%
- C) It is unchanged
- D) It increased by 2%
- E) It increased by 4%

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → −4%.
**explanation:** Successive % changes compound multiplicatively: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → 4% decrease. Smart number 100: 100 → 120 → 96.
**mistake_b:** Arithmetic error in 1.20 × 0.80: computed 0.98 instead of 0.96 (e.g., mentally calculated 120 × 80 = 9600 but placed the decimal at 0.980). The exact product: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → −4%.
**mistake_c:** Treated as additive (+20 − 20 = 0) → unchanged.
**mistake_d:** Inverted sign → +2%.
**mistake_e:** Inverted sign and rounded → +4%.
**common_trap:** Treating +x% then −x% as canceling. Pattern: net change is always a *loss* of (x/10)²%.
**takeaway:** Successive percent changes compound multiplicatively. +x% then −x% nets a 1 − x²/10000 change — always a loss.

## Q8
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixtures

A chemist has 30 liters of a 20% salt solution. How many liters of pure water must be added to produce a 15% salt solution?

- A) 5
- B) 7.5
- C) 10
- D) 12
- E) 15

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Salt fixed at 6 L. New total = 6/0.15 = 40 → add 10 L water.
**explanation:** Salt = 0.20 × 30 = 6 L (unchanged). New total: 6/0.15 = 40 L. Water added = 40 − 30 = 10 L.
**mistake_a:** Used a mangled proportion: (old% − target%) × volume ÷ combined% = (0.05 × 30) ÷ 0.30 = 5. Fix: lock onto fixed salt (6 L), set new total = 6 ÷ 0.15 = 40, added water = 10.
**mistake_b:** Divided by the original concentration instead of the target: (0.05 × 30) ÷ 0.20 = 1.5 ÷ 0.20 = 7.5. Use target% (0.15) as the divisor, not original% (0.20).
**mistake_d:** Estimated 1/0.15 ≈ 8 (using 0.125 as an approximation) and computed 1.5 × 8 = 12. Exact reciprocal: 1/0.15 = 6.67, giving 1.5 × 6.67 = 10.
**mistake_e:** Multiplied instead of divided in the final step: wrote 0.05 × 30 = 1.5, then 1.5 × 10 = 15 instead of 1.5 ÷ 0.15 = 10.
**common_trap:** Tracking concentration directly instead of locking onto the unchanging quantity (salt).
**takeaway:** In mixtures, lock onto what *doesn't change* (salt, here). The carrier (water) varies.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-05-word-problems

## Q9
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Profit/Markup/Discount

A retailer marks up a product by 40% over cost, then offers a 25% discount off the marked price. If the cost is $80, what is the retailer's profit on a sale?

- A) $4
- B) $8
- C) $12
- D) $16
- E) $20

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05 → 5% profit on $80 = $4.
**explanation:** Marked = 80 × 1.40 = 112. Sale = 112 × 0.75 = 84. Profit = 84 − 80 = 4. Or chain: 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05 → 5% net profit → $4.
**mistake_b:** Computed net multiplier correctly (1.05×) but read the profit as 10% of cost rather than 5%: 0.10 × 80 = 8. The multiplier 1.05 means 5% net profit, not 10%.
**mistake_c:** Subtracted percents additively: 40% − 25% = 15%, then 0.15 × 80 = 12. The 25% discount applies to the *marked price* (1.40 × 80 = 112), not to cost — so you cannot subtract raw percentages.
**mistake_d:** Computed 20% of 80 = 16 (took percent diff additively).
**mistake_e:** Computed 25% of 80 = 20 (used wrong base for discount).
**common_trap:** Subtracting percents (40 − 25 = 15%) ignores that the 25% discount is taken off the *marked* price, not cost.
**takeaway:** Multipliers chain: net = (1 + markup)(1 − discount). Each percent uses its own base.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-05-word-problems

## Q10
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Ratios

The ratio of a to b is 3:5 and the ratio of b to c is 4:7. What is the ratio of a to c?

- A) 3:7
- B) 12:35
- C) 15:28
- D) 12:28
- E) 21:20

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** Match b. Scale 3:5 → 12:20, scale 4:7 → 20:35. Now a:b:c = 12:20:35 → a:c = 12:35.
**explanation:** Chain ratios by matching b. a:b = 3:5 → 12:20 (×4). b:c = 4:7 → 20:35 (×5). Combined: a:b:c = 12:20:35 → a:c = 12:35.
**mistake_a:** Paired first numerator with last denominator (3:7) — wrong shortcut.
**mistake_c:** Scaled each ratio by a different factor (×5 gives a:b = 15:25; ×4 gives b:c = 16:28) but read a from the first and c from the second without matching b first: 15:28. The shared term b must equal 20 in both before you read across.
**mistake_d:** Mis-scaled c → 12:28.
**mistake_e:** Inverted ratio → 21:20.
**common_trap:** Pairing first numerator with last denominator (3:7) instead of properly scaling so b matches.
**takeaway:** To chain ratios, the shared term must be *equal* in both. Scale by LCM, then read across.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q11

**topic:** Percent Change

A company's revenue grew by 50% from 2023 to 2024. If 2024 revenue was $900,000, what was the 2023 revenue?

- A) $450,000
- B) $540,000
- C) $600,000
- D) $675,000
- E) $750,000

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 900K / 1.5 = 600K.
**explanation:** 2024 = 1.5 × 2023 → 2023 = 900,000 / 1.5 = 600,000.
**mistake_a:** Halved 900K → 450K (treated +50% as "half added").
**mistake_b:** Subtracted 40% of 900K → 540K.
**mistake_d:** Multiplied by 0.75 instead of dividing by 1.50: 900K × 0.75 = 675K. These are not equivalent — 1 ÷ 1.5 ≈ 0.667, not 0.75.
**mistake_e:** Subtracted 150K (some other percent) → 750K.
**common_trap:** Treating "+50% increase" as "half" — divides by 2 instead of by 1.5.
**takeaway:** To reverse a +x% increase, *divide* by (1 + x/100). Never subtract the percent.

A 12-liter mixture contains alcohol and water in the ratio 1:3. How many liters of alcohol must be added so that the ratio of alcohol to water becomes 2:3?

- A) 2
- B) 3
- C) 4
- D) 5
- E) 6

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** Water (9 L) is fixed. New alc = (2/3)(9) = 6. Added = 6 − 3 = 3.
**explanation:** Initially: alc = 3, water = 9 (ratio 1:3 of 12 L). Water is unchanged. New alc/water = 2/3 → alc = 6. Added = 6 − 3 = 3 L.

**mistake_d:** Set up the equation with total decreasing instead of increasing: x = (2/3)(12 − x) → 5x = 24 → x ≈ 4.8 ≈ 5. Error: adding alcohol *increases* the total, not decreases it. Water (9 L) is fixed → new total = 9/(3/5) = 15 → added = 3.
**mistake_e:** Computed new alc = 6, forgot to subtract original.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the *new* alcohol total (6) instead of the *added* amount.
**takeaway:** Lock onto the unchanging quantity (water). Compute the new value, then subtract the original.

## Q13
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

If 30% of x equals 45% of y, what is the ratio of x to y?

- A) 2:3
- B) 3:2
- C) 1:2
- D) 2:1
- E) 3:4

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** x/y = 0.45/0.30 = 3/2.
**explanation:** 0.30x = 0.45y → x/y = 0.45/0.30 = 3/2.
**mistake_a:** Inverted: 2:3 (matched percent ratio directly).
**mistake_c:** Correctly computed x/y = 30/45 = 2/3 but misread 2/3 as 1:2 (perhaps dividing numerator and denominator each by 2, treating 2/3 as "1 out of 2 parts").
**mistake_d:** Inverted the fraction: computed 0.30/0.45 = 2/3 rather than 0.45/0.30 = 3/2, then further rearranged to 2:1.
**mistake_e:** Arithmetic slip reducing 45:30 — wrote 3:4 instead of 3:2. Always verify: 45 ÷ 15 = 3, 30 ÷ 15 = 2, giving 3:2.
**common_trap:** Inverting the ratio (2:3) by reasoning "30% < 45%, so x < y" — ignores that *equal products* mean smaller percent ↔ larger quantity.
**takeaway:** When equal products: smaller percent ↔ larger quantity. 30%·x = 45%·y → x is the *bigger* quantity.

## Q14
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Ratios

In a bag of marbles containing only red and blue marbles, what is the number of red marbles?

(1) The ratio of red marbles to blue marbles is 3:5.
(2) There are 24 more blue marbles than red marbles.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) ALONE is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) ALONE is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** (1) ratio only — infinite scalings. (2) difference only — no ratio. Together: 5k − 3k = 24 → k = 12 → red = 36.
**explanation:** Statement (1): r:b = 3:5, so r = 3k for any k. Insufficient. Statement (2): b − r = 24, but no ratio. Insufficient. Together: 2k = 24 → k = 12 → red = 36. Sufficient.
**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_d:** Each alone — neither is.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient.
**common_trap:** Forgetting that a ratio alone gives infinite valid scalings.
**takeaway:** Ratio + any *one* scalar anchor (total, part, or difference) determines all parts.

## Q16
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixtures

A 50-liter mixture is 40% alcohol. A second mixture, which is 70% alcohol, is added to the first until the resulting mixture is 55% alcohol. How many liters of the second mixture were added?

- A) 25
- B) 40
- C) 50
- D) 60
- E) 75

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Alligation: target 55% is 15 from 40 and 15 from 70 → equal volumes → x = 50.
**explanation:** 0.40(50) + 0.70x = 0.55(50 + x) → 20 + 0.7x = 27.5 + 0.55x → 0.15x = 7.5 → x = 50. Alligation shortcut: 55% is equidistant from 40% and 70% → equal volumes.
**mistake_a:** Recognized that the target is equidistant (alligation shortcut) but computed "equal halves" of the *original*: 50/2 = 25. Equal distances mean the second volume equals the first (50 L), not half of it.
**mistake_b:** Rounded 0.55 × 50 = 27.5 down to 27 before completing the algebra: 20 + 0.70x = 27 + 0.55x → 0.15x = 6 → x = 40. Carry 27.5 exactly: 0.15x = 7.5 → x = 50.
**mistake_d:** Arithmetic error on the target-concentration term: computed 0.55 × 50 = 30 instead of 27.5, giving 0.15x = 9 → x = 60. Always verify: 0.55 × 50 = 27.5, and double-check with 55/100 × 50.
**mistake_e:** Applied alligation using concentration values rather than distances from the target: constructed a ratio of 70:40 or similar, arriving at x ≈ 75. The correct alligation: distance from 40% to 55% = 15; distance from 55% to 70% = 15 — equidistant, so volumes are equal (50 L each).
**common_trap:** Setting up the equation with the wrong base on one side, or skipping alligation when distances are equal.
**takeaway:** Alligation: distance ratios = volume ratios (inverted). Equidistant target → equal volumes.

## Q17
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Profit/Markup/Discount

A merchant sells a watch for $140 and earns a 30% profit on the selling price. If instead the watch had been sold at a 30% profit on the cost price, what would the selling price have been?

- A) $122.20
- B) $124.60
- C) $125.80
- D) $127.40
- E) $130.00

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** Cost = 0.70 × 140 = 98. New sell = 1.30 × 98 = 127.40.
**explanation:** "30% profit on selling price" → cost = 70% of price = 0.70 × 140 = 98. New scenario: 30% profit on cost → sell = 1.30 × 98 = 127.40.
**mistake_a:** Arithmetic error computing 98 × 1.30: obtained 122.20. Correct method: 98 × 1 + 98 × 0.30 = 98 + 29.40 = 127.40.
**mistake_b:** Arithmetic error computing 98 × 1.30: obtained 124.60. Use the complement: 100 × 1.30 − 2 × 1.30 = 130 − 2.60 = 127.40.
**mistake_c:** Arithmetic error computing 98 × 1.30: obtained 125.80. Verify the markup: 30% of 98 = 29.40 (not 27.80), so 98 + 29.40 = 127.40.
**mistake_e:** Estimated 130 (rough rounding).
**common_trap:** Treating "margin on price" and "markup on cost" as equivalent — they aren't; the *base* differs.
**takeaway:** Always identify the *base* of a percent. "Profit margin" → base is selling price; "markup" → base is cost.

## Q18

**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Mixtures

A tank contains a mixture of water and alcohol. What percent of the mixture is alcohol?

(1) If 10 liters of water were added, the mixture would be 20% alcohol.
(2) If 5 liters of alcohol were added, the mixture would be 40% alcohol.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) ALONE is not sufficient.

**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_d:** Each alone — neither is.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient.
**common_trap:** Counting "two facts" without checking that they yield two *independent* equations in the same variables.
**takeaway:** Two unknowns need two independent equations. A "fact" only counts if it adds a new equation, not a restatement.

## Q19

**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Successive Percent Changes

A merchant marks the price of a watch up 50% above cost, and during a sale discounts the marked price by 20%. If the final sale price is $84 more than cost, what is the cost of the watch?

- A) $420
- B) $480
- C) $504
- D) $540
- E) $280

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** 1.50 × 0.80 = 1.20 → 20% net profit. 0.20C = 84 → C = 420.
**explanation:** Marked = 1.50C. Sale = 1.50C × 0.80 = 1.20C. Profit = 0.20C = 84 → C = 420.
**mistake_b:** Computed 1.20C = 84 but then solved C = 84/1.20 = 70, then rounded up to 480. Correct: profit = 0.20C = 84, not sale price = 84.
**mistake_c:** Estimated C using a rough midpoint between A and D → 504. No valid calculation path; pure guessing error.
**mistake_d:** Computed sale price incorrectly as 1.30C (used 50% − 20% = 30% additive) and set 0.30C = 84 × (some factor) → 540. Close to the additive answer but with an arithmetic error.
**mistake_e:** Computed the net profit rate additively: 50% − 20% = 30%, then solved 0.30 × C = 84 → C = 280. The error: successive percent changes multiply (1.50 × 0.80 = 1.20), not subtract. The correct net is 20%, giving C = 420.
**common_trap:** Treating successive percent changes additively (50 − 20 = 30%), which gives the wrong base for profit.
**takeaway:** Chain percent changes multiplicatively. Net effect = product of factors, not sum of percents.

## Q20
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Ratio with Scalar Anchor

In a class, the ratio of boys to girls is 3 to 5. How many students are in the class?

(1) There are 12 more girls than boys.
(2) There are 30 girls in the class.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** (1) 5k − 3k = 12 → k = 6 → total = 48. (2) 5k = 30 → k = 6 → total = 48. Each alone.
**explanation:** Let b = 3k, g = 5k. Statement (1): 5k − 3k = 2k = 12 → k = 6 → total = 8k = 48. Sufficient. Statement (2): 5k = 30 → k = 6 → total = 48. Sufficient. Each alone → D.
**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_c:** Required both — neither needs the other.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient.
**common_trap:** Concluding "the ratio alone can't give total" (true) but missing that *each* statement supplies a scalar anchor.
**takeaway:** Ratio + any one scalar (sum, difference, or single part) → all parts determined.

A store raised the price of a product by 20%, then offered a 25% discount on the new, higher price. The final price is what percent of the original price?

- A) 90%
- B) 95%
- C) 100%
- D) 105%
- E) 110%

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** 1.20 × 0.75 = 0.90 → 90%.
**explanation:** Chain: 1.20 × 0.75 = 0.90 → final is 90% of original.
**mistake_b:** Used additive (+20 − 25 = −5%) → 95%.
**mistake_c:** Treated as cancellation → 100%.
**mistake_d:** Inverted sign → 105%.
**mistake_e:** Mis-chained → 110%.
**common_trap:** Treating successive percent changes additively (−5% → 95%). The 25% discount is taken off the *new* price, not the original.
**takeaway:** Successive percent changes multiply factors, not add percents.

## Q22
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Ratio — Total Given One Part

A jar contains red, blue, and green marbles in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5. If there are 24 blue marbles in the jar, how many marbles are in the jar in total?

- A) 48
- B) 60
- C) 66
- D) 72
- E) 84

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** 4 parts = 24 → 1 part = 6. Total = 12 parts = 72.
**explanation:** Blue corresponds to 4 parts. 4 parts = 24 → 1 part = 6. Total parts = 3 + 4 + 5 = 12 → 12 × 6 = 72.
**mistake_a:** Counted only red + blue (3 + 4 = 7 parts)... wait, 7 × 6 = 42 (not a choice) or red + green (3 + 5 = 8 parts): 8 × 6 = 48. Forgot the third color group.
**mistake_b:** Added the per-part unit value (6) to the blue ratio number (4) to get "10 parts," then scaled: 10 × 6 = 60. The total parts come from summing the ratio, not mixing ratio numbers with unit values: 3 + 4 + 5 = 12.
**mistake_c:** Arithmetic error summing ratio parts: wrote 3 + 4 + 5 = 11 instead of 12 → 11 × 6 = 66. Re-add explicitly: 3 + 4 = 7, then 7 + 5 = 12.
**mistake_e:** Used 14 total parts (perhaps misread the ratio as 3:4:7 or double-counted one group): 14 × 6 = 84. The given ratio is 3:4:5, summing to exactly 12 parts.
**common_trap:** Computing only some of the parts (e.g., red + blue = 7 parts) instead of all three.
**takeaway:** Sum *all* ratio parts before scaling. "Total" means total parts, not just the named ones.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q23
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Net Percent Change

A store raised and then lowered the price of a product. What was the net percent change in price?

(1) The price was first raised by 20% and then lowered by 20%.
(2) The final price is $144, while the original price was $150.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** (1) 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → −4%. (2) (144−150)/150 = −4%. Each alone.
**explanation:** Statement (1): 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → net −4%. Sufficient. Statement (2): (144 − 150)/150 = −4%. Sufficient. Each alone → D.
**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_c:** Required both.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient — but (1) is the classic "+x% then −x% → loss" pattern.
**common_trap:** Thinking +20% then −20% nets to 0 — missing the multiplicative compounding.
**takeaway:** +x% then −x% always nets a *loss* of (x/10)²% (here 4%). Memorize the pattern.

## Q24
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Successive Percent Growth

A town's population grew by 20% in 2022 and by an additional 25% in 2023. At the end of 2023, the town's population is what percent greater than it was at the end of 2021?

- A) 22.5%
- B) 45%
- C) 50%
- D) 55%
- E) 500%

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 1.20 × 1.25 = 1.50 → +50%.
**explanation:** Chain: 1.20 × 1.25 = 1.50 → 50% greater.
**mistake_a:** Averaged the two growth rates: (20 + 25) / 2 = 22.5%. Growth rates compound multiplicatively, not as averages. 1.20 × 1.25 = 1.50 → 50% net growth.
**mistake_b:** Used additive (+20 + 25 = 45%).
**mistake_d:** Added percentages but included the starting base: 100 + 20 + 25 = 145 → "45% growth" — then made an arithmetic error to reach 55%. Still a flawed additive approach; multiply the factors.
**mistake_e:** Mis-converted 1.50 → 500%.
**common_trap:** Adding the percentages (20 + 25 = 45%) instead of multiplying the factors.
**takeaway:** Compound growth: multiply (1 + r₁)(1 + r₂)... Always factors, never percentages.

## Q25
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Comparison via Chained Percent Statements

Is the price of product X greater than the price of product Y?

(1) Product X costs 20% more than product Z.
(2) Product Z costs 10% less than product Y.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** (1) X = 1.20Z, no Y. (2) Z = 0.90Y, no X. Together: X = 1.20 × 0.90 × Y = 1.08Y > Y.
**explanation:** Statement (1): X = 1.20Z, no Y info → insufficient. Statement (2): Z = 0.90Y, no X info → insufficient. Together: X = 1.20 × 0.90 × Y = 1.08Y > Y (positive prices). Definite YES → sufficient.
**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_d:** Each alone — neither is.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient — but the shared Z chains them.
**common_trap:** Thinking statements about "different products" can't combine — but a shared variable (Z) bridges them.
**takeaway:** Look for shared variables in DS statements. Statements that mention different things may still chain through a common reference.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations
