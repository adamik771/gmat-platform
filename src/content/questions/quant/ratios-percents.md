## Q1
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

What is 35% of 80?

- A) 24
- B) 26
- C) 28
- D) 32
- E) 35

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 10% of 80 = 8 → 30% = 24, +5% = 4 → 28.
**explanation:** 35% of 80 = 0.35 × 80 = 28. Faster: 30% (24) + 5% (4) = 28.
**mistake_a:** Computed 30% only (24) and stopped — forgot to add the 5% chunk (which is 4 more).
**mistake_b:** Computed 30% (= 24) and added 2 instead of 4 — likely misremembered 5% of 80 as 2 (5% of 40, not 80).
**mistake_d:** Computed 40% (= 32) instead of 35% — rounded the percent up to a "nice" number, ignoring the trailing 5%.
**mistake_e:** Bubbled the percent value (35) directly — confused "X% of Y" with the percent number itself.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the percent number itself, or computing only the round 30% portion.
**takeaway:** Break percents into 10% and 5% chunks: 10% = ÷10, 5% = ÷20. Faster than decimals.

## Q2
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

If 18 is x percent of 60, what is the value of x?

- A) 18
- B) 24
- C) 30
- D) 36
- E) 42

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 18/60 = 3/10 = 30%.
**explanation:** 18 = (x/100)(60) → x = 1800/60 = 30. Or: 18/60 = 3/10 = 30%.
**mistake_a:** Bubbled the part value (18) directly — read "what is x" but answered with the number 18.
**mistake_b:** Computed 24 from a wrong base — likely 18 × 60/45 or some inverted setup landing near 24%. Recompute: 18/60 reduces to 3/10 = 30%, not 24%.
**mistake_d:** Computed 60 × 0.60 = 36 from misreading the question as "60% of x = 18" or similar inversion.
**mistake_e:** Computed the *complement*: 60 − 18 = 42, treating the question as "what percent of 60 is *everything else*." The question asks what 18 is of 60, not the remainder.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the part (18) instead of computing the percent.
**takeaway:** Memorize fraction ↔ percent: 1/4 = 25%, 3/10 = 30%, 3/8 = 37.5%, 5/8 = 62.5%. Recognition saves time.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q3
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Ratios

A box contains only red and green pens in the ratio 4:7. If there are 44 pens in total, how many green pens are in the box?

- A) 16
- B) 22
- C) 24
- D) 28
- E) 32

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** 4 + 7 = 11 parts. 44/11 = 4 per part. Green = 7 × 4 = 28.
**explanation:** Ratio parts sum to 11 → each part = 44/11 = 4. Green = 7 × 4 = 28.
**mistake_a:** Bubbled the *red* count (4 × 4 = 16) — solved correctly but for the wrong color.
**mistake_b:** Bubbled half the total (22) — defaulted to "split-evenly" instead of using the ratio.
**mistake_c:** Computed 11 parts but mis-scaled: 44/11 = 4, then multiplied by 6 (split the difference between 4 and 7) → 24. Always multiply by the actual part value.
**mistake_e:** Used 44/(4+7+1) ≈ 4 per part, then 7 × 4.6 ≈ 32 — sloppy division on the part count. Or 44 − 12 (red) = 32, mis-computing red as 12.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the *other* color (red, 16) by mis-reading the question.
**takeaway:** Identify which quantity the question asks for *before* computing. Always confirm before bubbling.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q4
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Percent Change

A jacket originally priced at $120 is marked down by 25%. What is the sale price?

- A) $80
- B) $90
- C) $95
- D) $96
- E) $100

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** 0.75 × 120 = 90.
**explanation:** 25% off → pay 75%. 0.75 × 120 = 90. Or: 25% of 120 = 30; 120 − 30 = 90.
**mistake_a:** Mis-translated 25% as 1/3 — computed 120 − 40 = 80. The canonical fraction trap: 25% is 1/4 (giving 30 off), not 1/3.
**mistake_c:** Computed 120 − 25 = 95 — treated the 25 as a *dollar* amount instead of a percent. The discount is 25% of 120, not $25.
**mistake_d:** Computed 120 × 0.80 = 96 — used a 20% discount factor (0.80) instead of 25% (0.75). One-digit slip with a big swing.
**mistake_e:** Computed 20% off ($24) → $96, then mis-rounded to $100, or interpreted the discount as $20 flat off. Read the percent twice.
**common_trap:** Translating 25% as 1/3 (gives 80) instead of 1/4.
**takeaway:** Use multipliers for percent changes: −x% → multiply by (1 − x/100). One-step.

## Q5
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Proportions

On a map, 2 inches represents 15 actual miles. If two cities are 7 inches apart on the map, how many actual miles apart are they?

- A) 30
- B) 45
- C) 52.5
- D) 60
- E) 105

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 7/2 × 15 = 3.5 × 15 = 52.5 mi.
**explanation:** Proportion 2/15 = 7/m → m = 7 × 15/2 = 105/2 = 52.5 mi.
**mistake_a:** Computed 2 × 15 = 30 — used the map-inches value as the multiplier instead of the actual-inches-on-map. The scaling factor is 7/2, not 2.
**mistake_b:** Computed 3 × 15 = 45 — rounded 7/2 down to 3 instead of keeping 3.5. Rounding mid-proportion costs precision.
**mistake_d:** Computed 4 × 15 = 60 — rounded 7/2 up to 4. Same rounding error in the other direction.
**mistake_e:** Forgot to divide by 2: 7 × 15 = 105. Treated the proportion as 1 inch = 15 miles instead of 2 inches = 15 miles.
**common_trap:** Forgetting the divisor (2 inches) and multiplying 7 × 15 = 105.
**takeaway:** Cross-multiply with units labeled (inches/inches = miles/miles). Prevents flipping.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q6
**difficulty:** Easy
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

In a class of 40 students, 14 are left-handed. What percent of the class is left-handed?

- A) 28%
- B) 32%
- C) 35%
- D) 38%
- E) 40%

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 14/40 = 7/20 = 35/100 = 35%.
**explanation:** Percent = part/whole × 100 = 14/40 × 100 = 35%. Or: 14/40 = 7/20, and 7/20 × 5/5 = 35/100 = 35%.
**mistake_a:** Computed 14 × 2 = 28% — mis-converted by doubling instead of multiplying by 2.5. 14/40 = 7/20; converting /20 to /100 requires × 5, not × 4.
**mistake_b:** Computed 32% from a fraction slip — perhaps 14/40 simplified to 8/25 wrongly, or 12/40 misread as 14/40 mid-step. Recompute: 14/40 = 35/100.
**mistake_d:** Computed 38% — likely 15/40 = 37.5% rounded up, or 14/37 from a denominator mis-read.
**mistake_e:** Bubbled the class size (40) as a percent — confused the *whole* with the percent it represents.
**common_trap:** Mis-converting /20 fractions to /100 (× 5, not × 4 or × 6).
**takeaway:** /20 fractions: × 5 to convert to percent. /25: × 4. Memorize denominators that convert cleanly.

## Q7
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Percent Change

The price of a stock increases by 20% in January, then decreases by 20% in February. By what percent has the stock's price changed from the start of January to the end of February?

- A) It decreased by 4%
- B) It decreased by 2%
- C) It is unchanged
- D) It increased by 2%
- E) It increased by 4%

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → −4%.
**explanation:** Successive % changes compound multiplicatively: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → 4% decrease. Smart number 100: 100 → 120 → 96.
**mistake_b:** Mis-computed compound → −2%.
**mistake_c:** Treated as additive (+20 − 20 = 0) → unchanged.
**mistake_d:** Inverted sign → +2%.
**mistake_e:** Inverted sign and rounded → +4%.
**common_trap:** Treating +x% then −x% as canceling. Pattern: net change is always a *loss* of (x/10)²%.
**takeaway:** Successive percent changes compound multiplicatively. +x% then −x% nets a 1 − x²/10000 change — always a loss.

## Q8
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixtures

A chemist has 30 liters of a 20% salt solution. How many liters of pure water must be added to produce a 15% salt solution?

- A) 5
- B) 7.5
- C) 10
- D) 12
- E) 15

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Salt fixed at 6 L. New total = 6/0.15 = 40 → add 10 L water.
**explanation:** Salt = 0.20 × 30 = 6 L (unchanged). New total: 6/0.15 = 40 L. Water added = 40 − 30 = 10 L.
**mistake_a:** Computed 5 from a halving shortcut — treated "drop from 20% to 15%" as halving the change (5 of 30 is roughly 1/6, near "half of 5%"). The salt-conservation method gives an exact 10, not a near-5.
**mistake_b:** Computed 7.5 from dividing 30 by 4 — treating the concentration drop as "1/4 of the volume." The correct math uses fixed salt: 6/0.15 = 40 → add 10.
**mistake_d:** Computed 12 from solving (0.20)(30) = (0.15)(30 + x) with a sign error → x ≈ 12 instead of 10. Recompute: 6 = 0.15(30+x) → 30+x = 40 → x = 10.
**mistake_e:** Computed 15 as 30 × 0.5 — defaulted to "halve the volume" thinking. The concentration only drops by a quarter (20→15, a 25% relative drop), so the volume change is smaller.
**common_trap:** Tracking concentration directly instead of locking onto the unchanging quantity (salt).
**takeaway:** In mixtures, lock onto what *doesn't change* (salt, here). The carrier (water) varies.

## Q9
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Profit/Markup/Discount

A retailer marks up a product by 40% over cost, then offers a 25% discount off the marked price. If the cost is $80, what is the retailer's profit on a sale?

- A) $4
- B) $8
- C) $12
- D) $16
- E) $20

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05 → 5% profit on $80 = $4.
**explanation:** Marked = 80 × 1.40 = 112. Sale = 112 × 0.75 = 84. Profit = 84 − 80 = 4. Or chain: 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05 → 5% net profit → $4.
**mistake_b:** Computed 10% of 80 = 8, treating the net profit margin as half the markup percent. The exact net margin is 1.40 × 0.75 − 1 = 5%, giving $4 — not 10%.
**mistake_c:** Computed 15% of 80 = 12 — used the additive shortcut (40 − 25 = 15%). That treats the discount as a flat 25% off cost, but it's 25% off the *marked* price.
**mistake_d:** Computed 20% of 80 = 16 — treated the markup-minus-discount as 20% (perhaps via 40 − 20 from a misread). The correct net is 5%.
**mistake_e:** Computed 25% of 80 = 20 — applied the discount percent to cost instead of marked price. The 25% comes off $112 (= 1.40 × 80), giving $28 off, not 25% of cost.
**common_trap:** Subtracting percents (40 − 25 = 15%) ignores that the 25% discount is taken off the *marked* price, not cost.
**takeaway:** Multipliers chain: net = (1 + markup)(1 − discount). Each percent uses its own base.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-05-word-problems

## Q10
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Ratios

The ratio of a to b is 3:5 and the ratio of b to c is 4:7. What is the ratio of a to c?

- A) 3:7
- B) 12:35
- C) 15:28
- D) 12:28
- E) 21:20

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** Match b. Scale 3:5 → 12:20, scale 4:7 → 20:35. Now a:b:c = 12:20:35 → a:c = 12:35.
**explanation:** Chain ratios by matching b. a:b = 3:5 → 12:20 (×4). b:c = 4:7 → 20:35 (×5). Combined: a:b:c = 12:20:35 → a:c = 12:35.
**mistake_a:** Paired first numerator (3) with last denominator (7) directly — the false shortcut "outer terms only." Ratio chaining requires scaling so the shared term (b) matches in both ratios.
**mistake_c:** Computed 15:28 by reversing the scaling — used 3 × 5 = 15 for a, but kept c at 7 × 4 = 28 instead of 35. The cross-multiplication only works after b is unified.
**mistake_d:** Computed 12:28 — scaled a correctly to 12 but used c = 28 (from 7 × 4) instead of 35. The c-side has to scale by 5 (the b-multiplier from a:b), not 4.
**mistake_e:** Inverted the ratio: 21:20 — multiplied a by c's coefficient and vice versa. The chain a→b→c must preserve direction.
**common_trap:** Pairing first numerator with last denominator (3:7) instead of properly scaling so b matches.
**takeaway:** To chain ratios, the shared term must be *equal* in both. Scale by LCM, then read across.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

## Q11
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Percent Change

A company's revenue grew by 50% from 2023 to 2024. If 2024 revenue was $900,000, what was the 2023 revenue?

- A) $450,000
- B) $540,000
- C) $600,000
- D) $675,000
- E) $750,000

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 900K / 1.5 = 600K.
**explanation:** 2024 = 1.5 × 2023 → 2023 = 900,000 / 1.5 = 600,000.
**mistake_a:** Halved 900K → 450K — treated "+50% increase" as "doubled," then divided by 2. A +50% increase means *multiply* by 1.5, not 2.
**mistake_b:** Subtracted 40% of 900K (360K) → 540K. Reverse-percent error: subtracted instead of dividing by the multiplier.
**mistake_d:** Computed 900K × 0.75 = 675K — divided by 4/3 instead of by 3/2, perhaps confused which multiplier reverses +50%.
**mistake_e:** Subtracted 150K (one-sixth of 900K) → 750K, defaulting to "subtract the percent of the new value." The correct reverse: 2023 = 2024 / 1.5 = 600K.
**common_trap:** Treating "+50% increase" as "half" — divides by 2 instead of by 1.5.
**takeaway:** To reverse a +x% increase, *divide* by (1 + x/100). Never subtract the percent.
**related_reading:** reading-quant-02-arithmetic-foundations

A 12-liter mixture contains alcohol and water in the ratio 1:3. How many liters of alcohol must be added so that the ratio of alcohol to water becomes 2:3?

- B) 3
- C) 4

**answer:** B

**mistake_d:** Computed 5 from solving (3+x)/9 = 2/3 incorrectly — perhaps 3+x = 6 then off-by-one. Recompute: (3+x)/9 = 2/3 → 3+x = 6 → x = 3.
**mistake_e:** Computed new alc = 6 correctly, but bubbled the *new total* instead of the *amount added*. The question asks how much to add, not the final amount.
**common_trap:** Bubbling the *new* alcohol total (6) instead of the *added* amount.
**takeaway:** Lock onto the unchanging quantity (water). Compute the new value, then subtract the original.

## Q13
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Basic Percents

If 30% of x equals 45% of y, what is the ratio of x to y?

- A) 2:3
- B) 3:2
- C) 1:2
- D) 2:1
- E) 3:4

**answer:** B
**fastest_path:** x/y = 0.45/0.30 = 3/2.
**explanation:** Translate the equality: 0.30x = 0.45y → x/y = 0.45/0.30 = 3/2. Sanity check: a *smaller* fraction (30%) of x matches a *larger* fraction (45%) of y, so x has to be the larger quantity → x/y > 1. Only B and D satisfy that; the exact 3/2 lands on B.
**mistake_a:** Inverted the ratio: matched the percentages directly (30:45 = 2:3) instead of solving for x/y. Percent ratios don't translate one-to-one to quantity ratios when the products are equal.
**mistake_c:** Computed x/y as 1:2 — perhaps inverted both: 30/45 = 2/3, then halved by mistake. Always solve for the variable explicitly: x/y = 0.45/0.30, not 0.30/0.45.
**mistake_d:** Computed x/y = 2:1 from a sign or operator slip — close to the right magnitude but landed at 2 instead of 3/2. Recompute exactly: 0.45/0.30 = 1.5, not 2.
**mistake_e:** Used 3:4 from a fraction simplification mistake — perhaps 30/40 from misreading "45" as "40" mid-computation. Read the percents twice before dividing.
**common_trap:** Inverting the ratio (2:3) by reasoning "30% < 45%, so x < y" — ignores that *equal products* mean smaller percent ↔ larger quantity.
**takeaway:** When equal products: smaller percent ↔ larger quantity. 30%·x = 45%·y → x is the *bigger* quantity.

## Q14
**difficulty:** Medium
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Ratios

In a bag of marbles containing only red and blue marbles, what is the number of red marbles?

(1) The ratio of red marbles to blue marbles is 3:5.
(2) There are 24 more blue marbles than red marbles.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) ALONE is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) ALONE is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** (1) ratio only — infinite scalings. (2) difference only — no ratio. Together: 5k − 3k = 24 → k = 12 → red = 36.
**explanation:** Statement (1): r:b = 3:5, so r = 3k for any k. Insufficient. Statement (2): b − r = 24, but no ratio. Insufficient. Together: 2k = 24 → k = 12 → red = 36. Sufficient.
**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_d:** Each alone — neither is.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient.
**common_trap:** Forgetting that a ratio alone gives infinite valid scalings.
**takeaway:** Ratio + any *one* scalar anchor (total, part, or difference) determines all parts.

## Q16

**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixtures

A 50-liter mixture is 40% alcohol. A second mixture, which is 70% alcohol, is added to the first until the resulting mixture is 55% alcohol. How many liters of the second mixture were added?

- A) 25
- B) 40
- C) 50
- D) 60
- E) 75

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** Alligation: target 55% is 15 from 40 and 15 from 70 → equal volumes → x = 50.
**explanation:** 0.40(50) + 0.70x = 0.55(50 + x) → 20 + 0.7x = 27.5 + 0.55x → 0.15x = 7.5 → x = 50. Alligation shortcut: 55% is equidistant from 40% and 70% → equal volumes.
**mistake_a:** Computed 25 from halving the answer — perhaps reasoned "halfway between 40% and 70% so half the volume." The alligation gives equal volumes (50 of each), not half.
**mistake_b:** Computed 40 from setting up 0.40(50) + 0.70x = 0.55(x) (forgot the +50 in the denominator). Recompute: total volume after addition is 50 + x, not x.
**mistake_d:** Computed 60 from arithmetic slip in the algebra: 0.15x = 9 instead of 7.5 → x = 60. The right hand side is 27.5 − 20 = 7.5, not 9.
**mistake_e:** Computed 75 by setting target = 60% instead of 55% — misread the target concentration. Recompute with 55%: 0.15x = 7.5 → x = 50.
**common_trap:** Setting up the equation with the wrong base on one side, or skipping alligation when distances are equal.
**takeaway:** Alligation: distance ratios = volume ratios (inverted). Equidistant target → equal volumes.

- D) $127.40

**explanation:** "30% profit on selling price" → cost = 70% of price = 0.70 × 140 = 98. New scenario: 30% profit on cost → sell = 1.30 × 98 = 127.40.
**mistake_a:** Used the wrong cost: 140 × 0.873 ≈ 122.20 — perhaps computed cost as 140/1.30 ≈ 107.7, then 1.13 × ... a confused chain. The correct cost is 140 × 0.70 = 98 (since profit is 30% of *selling price*).

**mistake_e:** Estimated $130 by rough-rounding cost to $100 and adding 30% — close to the correct answer but imprecise. Compute exactly: 1.30 × 98 = 127.40, not 130.
**common_trap:** Treating "margin on price" and "markup on cost" as equivalent — they aren't; the *base* differs.

## Q18

**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Mixtures

A tank contains a mixture of water and alcohol. What percent of the mixture is alcohol?

(1) If 10 liters of water were added, the mixture would be 20% alcohol.
(2) If 5 liters of alcohol were added, the mixture would be 40% alcohol.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) ALONE is not sufficient.

- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.

**mistake_a:** Treated only (1) as sufficient.
**mistake_b:** Treated only (2) as sufficient.
**mistake_d:** Each alone — neither is.
**mistake_e:** Concluded both insufficient.
**common_trap:** Counting "two facts" without checking that they yield two *independent* equations in the same variables.
**takeaway:** Two unknowns need two independent equations. A "fact" only counts if it adds a new equation, not a restatement.

**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Successive Percent Changes

A merchant marks the price of a watch up 50% above cost, and during a sale discounts the marked price by 20%. If the final sale price is $84 more than cost, what is the cost of the watch?

- A) $420
- B) $480
- C) $504

**topic:** Ratio vs. Count

What fraction of the students in a class passed the exam?

(1) The ratio of students who passed to students who failed is 3:1.
(2) Exactly 36 students passed the exam.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) ALONE is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) ALONE is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** A
**fastest_path:** (1) 3:1 → passed = 3/(3+1) = 3/4 of the class. Sufficient. (2) 36 passed, total unknown → fraction unknown. Insufficient.
**explanation:** Statement (1): passed:failed = 3:1, so passed = 3/4 of total. The *fraction* is determined by the ratio alone — no actual count needed. Sufficient. Statement (2): 36 students passed, but without knowing the total (or the failed count), the fraction passed is unknown. Insufficient. Answer A.
**mistake_b:** Incorrectly treated Statement (2) as sufficient — 36 tells us a count, not a fraction; without a total or the failed count, no fraction can be computed.
**mistake_c:** Required both statements — the key insight is that a part-to-part ratio *determines* the part-to-whole fraction. 3:1 → 3/4 is fully determined by (1) alone.
**mistake_d:** Tried to make both sufficient — (2) requires additional info (total or failed count) and cannot produce a fraction by itself.
**mistake_e:** Claimed both insufficient — (1) unambiguously yields fraction = 3/4.
**common_trap:** Thinking a ratio is useless without a count — but a ratio between parts *fully determines* the fraction of the whole, even without knowing actual counts.
**takeaway:** Part-to-part ratio → part-to-whole fraction requires no count. 3:1 → 3/4, always. Counts are only needed when an absolute number is requested.

## Q38
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Successive Discounts

A retailer lists a product at $120. During a clearance event, the retailer first applies a 25% discount and then applies an additional 10% discount on the reduced price. What is the total percent reduction from the original price?

- A) 30%
- B) 31.5%
- C) 32.5%
- D) 33%
- E) 35%

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** 0.75 × 0.90 = 0.675 → 32.5% reduction.
**explanation:** First discount: $120 × 0.75 = $90. Second discount: $90 × 0.90 = $81. Reduction = (120 − 81)/120 = 39/120 = 32.5%. Or chain multipliers: 0.75 × 0.90 = 0.675, so final = 67.5% of original, reduction = 32.5%.
**mistake_a:** Added the discounts: 25 + 10 = 35%, then rounded down to 30%. Successive discounts multiply, not add — and 35% overstates the reduction.
**mistake_b:** Computed 25 + 10 − (25 × 10)/100 = 35 − 2.5 = 32.5% but then selected 31.5% from an arithmetic slip. The formula 1 − (1 − a)(1 − b) = a + b − ab gives exactly 32.5%.
**mistake_d:** Added 25 + 10 = 35%, subtracted 1.5 (a mistaken interaction term), landed at 33%. The exact interaction is (0.25)(0.10) × 120 = $3, which contributes exactly 2.5 percentage points of the total reduction.
**mistake_e:** Added the two discounts directly: 25 + 10 = 35%. This is always wrong; the second discount applies to the already-reduced price.
**common_trap:** Adding the two discount percents (25 + 10 = 35%) as though both apply to the original price.
**takeaway:** Successive discounts: multiply the keep-fractions. Total reduction = 1 − (1 − d₁)(1 − d₂). Never add discount percents.

## Q39
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Mixture — Removal and Replacement

A 40-liter container holds a mixture that is 25% juice and 75% water. Eight liters of the mixture are removed and replaced with 8 liters of pure juice. What percent of the resulting mixture is juice?

- A) 30%
- B) 35%
- C) 37.5%
- D) 40%
- E) 45%

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** Juice initially = 10 L. Remove 8 L of mixture (= 2 L juice). Add 8 L pure juice. Final juice = 10 − 2 + 8 = 16 L in 40 L total = 40%.
**explanation:** Initial juice = 0.25 × 40 = 10 L, water = 30 L. Remove 8 L of 25% mixture: remove 0.25 × 8 = 2 L juice and 6 L water. Remaining: juice = 8, water = 24 (total 32 L). Add 8 L pure juice: juice = 16, water = 24, total = 40 L. Percent juice = 16/40 = 40%.
**mistake_a:** Removed 8 L of pure juice instead of mixture: 10 − 8 = 2, then +8 = 10, 10/40 = 25%... then estimated "net gain" as 30%. The removed liquid is the mixture (25% juice), not pure juice.
**mistake_b:** Computed 35% from subtracting 8 L of pure water (rather than mixture) then adding 8 L juice: (10 − 0 + 8)/40 = 18/40 = 45%, then averaged with 25% → 35%. Both steps are wrong.
**mistake_c:** Computed 15/40 = 37.5% — removed the full 8 L as juice (10 − 8 = 2), added 8 back (= 10), then miscounted the total as 40 but used 15 numerator. Recompute carefully: removed liquid is the *mixture*, so only 2 L of juice leaves.
**mistake_e:** Computed 18/40 = 45% — forgot that removing the mixture also removes some juice. Treated the 8 L removal as removing only water (juice stays at 10), then added 8 L juice → 18 L. The 8 L removed contains 25% juice (= 2 L).
**common_trap:** Treating the removed quantity as pure water — forgetting that the *mixture* is removed, so juice leaves proportionally.
**takeaway:** In removal-and-replacement: removed liquid = same concentration as the current mixture. Always track juice and water separately through each step.

## Q40
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Data Sufficiency
**topic:** Three-Part Ratio — Finding a Count

A bag contains only red, blue, and green marbles. How many blue marbles are in the bag?

(1) The ratio of red to blue to green marbles is 2:3:5.
(2) There are 30 more green marbles than red marbles.

- A) Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) ALONE is not sufficient.
- B) Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) ALONE is not sufficient.
- C) BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient.
- D) EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
- E) Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** (1) 2:3:5, any k — infinite solutions. (2) green − red = 30, no ratio — infinite solutions. Together: 5k − 2k = 30 → k = 10 → blue = 30. Sufficient.
**explanation:** Statement (1): red = 2k, blue = 3k, green = 5k. Any positive integer k works. Insufficient. Statement (2): green − red = 30, but without a ratio, any split satisfying that difference is valid. Insufficient. Together: from (1), green − red = 5k − 2k = 3k. From (2), 3k = 30 → k = 10 → blue = 3 × 10 = 30. Sufficient. Answer C.
**mistake_a:** Treated (1) as sufficient because a ratio "sounds complete." A ratio gives relative amounts only; without a scalar anchor, infinitely many integer solutions exist.
**mistake_b:** Treated (2) as sufficient — the difference alone (green − red = 30) could be satisfied by red = 0, green = 30, blue = anything. No ratio, no unique blue count.
**mistake_d:** Both alone sufficient — neither is; each gives one constraint for two or three unknowns.
**mistake_e:** Both together insufficient — together they yield a unique solution: k = 10, blue = 30.
**common_trap:** Assuming that a complete three-way ratio is sufficient to find a count. The ratio is sufficient to find *fractions* (e.g., blue is 3/10 of total), but an absolute count requires one scalar anchor.
**takeaway:** Ratio + one scalar anchor (total, one part, or one difference) uniquely determines all parts. Any one of those anchors, combined with the ratio, is sufficient for a count.

## Q41
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Changing Percent of Total

A factory has two production lines. Line A currently produces 60% of total output and Line B produces 40%. Line A's daily output then increases by 50% and Line B's daily output decreases by 25%. After these changes, what percent of the new total daily output does Line A produce?

- A) 60%
- B) 65%
- C) 70%
- D) 75%
- E) 80%

**answer:** D
**fastest_path:** Let total = 100. A = 60 → 90. B = 40 → 30. New total = 120. A's share = 90/120 = 75%.
**explanation:** Assign current total = 100. Line A = 60, Line B = 40. New Line A = 60 × 1.50 = 90. New Line B = 40 × 0.75 = 30. New total = 90 + 30 = 120. A's share = 90/120 = 3/4 = 75%.
**mistake_a:** Left the percentages unchanged — didn't apply the output changes at all. The new shares must be recomputed from the changed absolute outputs.
**mistake_b:** Applied the percent changes to the *shares* directly: 60% × 1.50 = 90%, then averaged with B somehow → 65%. Percent changes apply to *output volumes*, not to *share percentages*.
**mistake_c:** Kept Line B fixed (forgot to apply −25%): A = 90, B = 40, total = 130. A's share = 90/130 ≈ 69%, rounded to 70%. Line B's output did change — apply its multiplier 0.75.
**mistake_e:** Applied a rough additive estimate: A's share goes up by "roughly 50% of 60% = 30 percentage points," giving 90%, averaged down to 80%. The correct path uses absolute outputs, not percentage-point arithmetic.
**common_trap:** Forgetting to apply Line B's change (−25%) and computing 90/(90+40) = 69% instead of 90/(90+30) = 75%.
**takeaway:** When shares change, always convert to absolute quantities (using smart numbers), apply each change, recompute the new total, then find the new share. Never apply percent changes to existing percentages.

## Q42
**difficulty:** Hard
**type:** Problem Solving
**topic:** Multi-Step Ratio + Percent Change

Two divisions, A and B, split a company's earnings in the ratio 3:2. Division A's earnings then increase by 40%, and Division B's earnings decrease by some percent. If the new ratio of A's earnings to B's earnings is 7:3, by what percent did Division B's earnings decrease?

- A) 5%
- B) 8%
- C) 10%
- D) 12%
- E) 15%

**answer:** C
**fastest_path:** A_new = 3k × 1.40 = 4.2k. B_new = 4.2k × (3/7) = 1.8k. Decrease = (2k − 1.8k)/2k = 10%.
**explanation:** Let original A = 3k, B = 2k. After increase: A_new = 3k × 1.40 = 4.2k. From new ratio 7:3: B_new = A_new × (3/7) = 4.2k × 3/7 = 1.8k. Percent decrease in B = (2k − 1.8k) / 2k = 0.2k / 2k = 10%.
**mistake_a:** Computed 5% by dividing the change (0.2k) by the total original earnings (4k) instead of by B's original value (2k): 0.2k / 4k = 5%. Always divide by the quantity whose percent change is asked — here, Division B's original earnings.
**mistake_b:** Computed 8% from a rounding or off-by-one error in the algebra. Exactly: B_new = 4.2k × 3/7 = 12.6k/7 = 1.8k, giving (2 − 1.8)/2 = 10%.
**mistake_d:** Computed (2k − 1.8k)/1.8k = 0.2/1.8 ≈ 11.1% ≈ 12% — divided by the *new* value instead of the *original* value. Percent decrease always uses the original as the denominator.
**mistake_e:** Estimated 15% by treating the ratio change as a 15-percentage-point shift without setting up algebra. A new ratio of 7:3 vs old ratio of 3:2 does not directly give a 15% drop in B.
**common_trap:** Using the new value as the denominator for percent change (→12%) or using the total as the denominator (→5%), rather than B's original earnings.
**takeaway:** Identify what the problem calls the "original" and "new" values for the quantity asked. Percent change = (original − new) / original, with the denominator always being the *original* value of that specific quantity.
